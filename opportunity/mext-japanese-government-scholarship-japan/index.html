MEXT Scholarship Overview

The Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship is administered by the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT). It is one of the world’s most generous government scholarship programmes, funding international students at Japanese universities across all academic levels and disciplines. Since its inception, MEXT has supported over 100,000 students from more than 160 countries.

MEXT is structured as a family of scholarship tracks rather than a single programme. The main tracks are undergraduate (five years including a one-year preparatory Japanese language course), master’s (two years), doctoral (three years), and research student (short-term to two years for non-degree research). All tracks share the same comprehensive funding model and emphasis on language preparation.

Applicants can apply through two distinct pathways: embassy recommendation and university recommendation. Each pathway has a different process, timeline, and level of competition. Your choice of pathway depends on your academic background, research readiness, and whether you have already identified a Japanese university and supervisor you wish to work with.

The MEXT scholarship is the most direct route to studying in Japan with full financial support. For a broader overview of study options in Japan, see our complete Study in Japan guide.

Funding and Benefits

MEXT is one of the few scholarships that provides truly comprehensive funding for the entire duration of your degree. There are no separate applications for tuition coverage or living costs — everything is included in a single scholarship award.

How to Apply Step by Step

Step 1: Choose Your Track

Decide whether embassy recommendation or university recommendation is right for you. If you already have a Japanese university and professor in mind, the university track may be better. If you want a structured process with clear timelines, the embassy track is more straightforward.

Step 2: Prepare Your Documents

Gather all required documents well in advance. The research proposal is the most important document for graduate applicants — it must be specific, well-structured, and aligned with the research interests of faculty at your target university.

Step 3: Submit Your Application

For the embassy track, submit your application to the Japanese embassy or consulate in your home country. For the university track, submit directly to the international admissions office of your chosen university.

Step 4: Take the Exam and Interview

Embassy track applicants take a written examination covering English, Japanese (if applicable), and academic subjects. Shortlisted candidates proceed to an interview. University track applicants are evaluated on their application package and may have an interview with the prospective supervisor.

Step 5: Receive Your Results

Successful candidates receive a Letter of Acceptance and begin the visa application process. The university assists with the Certificate of Eligibility required for the student visa.

Step 6: Prepare for Departure

Book your flight (covered by MEXT), arrange housing, and attend pre-departure orientations if offered by the embassy or university.

Selection Process

MEXT selection is rigorous and competitive at every stage. The embassy track involves an initial document screening, followed by a written examination in English, Japanese, and academic subjects relevant to your field. Candidates who pass the written exam proceed to an interview with a selection committee at the embassy.

The university track selection is managed by the receiving university. The admissions committee evaluates academic records, research proposals, recommendation letters, and language proficiency. The university then nominates selected candidates to MEXT for final approval.

Selection criteria include academic excellence, the quality and feasibility of the research proposal (for graduate applicants), language proficiency, and the candidate’s potential to contribute to Japan’s academic or professional community.

Tips for a Strong MEXT Application

  • Start early. MEXT applications require extensive preparation, especially the research proposal. Begin at least three to four months before the deadline.
  • Contact a professor. For graduate and research tracks, identifying and contacting a professor at your target university significantly increases your chances, especially for the university recommendation track.
  • Write a focused research proposal. Your proposal should clearly state your research question, methodology, expected outcomes, and how it aligns with the professor’s work. Be specific rather than broad.
  • Learn basic Japanese. Even if the programme is in English, demonstrating basic Japanese language ability shows commitment and will help with daily life and cultural integration.
  • Apply to multiple embassies or universities if possible. Some candidates apply simultaneously to multiple countries where they hold nationality or residency. Check the rules carefully.
  • Prepare for the written exam. The embassy track exam tests English, Japanese (if applicable), and academic subjects. Past exam papers are available online and are essential study material.

Questions and Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the MEXT scholarship cover?

MEXT is fully funded. It covers full tuition and entrance fees at the host Japanese university, round-trip international airfare, and a monthly living stipend of approximately ¥143,000 to ¥147,000 depending on the level of study.

Can I choose any university with MEXT?

Through the embassy recommendation track, MEXT assigns you to a university after screening, though you can express preferences. Through the university recommendation track, you apply directly to a specific university and professor.

What is the difference between embassy and university recommendation?

Embassy recommendation involves applying through the Japanese embassy in your home country with exams and interviews. University recommendation involves applying directly to a Japanese university, which then nominates you to MEXT. University recommendation requires contacting a professor first.

Do I need to speak Japanese for the MEXT scholarship?

Not initially, but MEXT includes up to six months of preparatory Japanese language training for most tracks. Some English-taught programmes require IELTS or TOEFL instead. Strong Japanese ability strengthens your application.

What is the age limit for MEXT scholarship applicants?

Undergraduate applicants generally need to be aged 17 to 25. Master’s, doctoral, and research student applicants are generally expected to be under 35 at the time of application.

When is the MEXT scholarship deadline 2026?

Embassy recommendation deadlines vary by country but typically fall between March and May each year. University recommendation deadlines depend on the specific university. Check the Japanese embassy in your country or your target university for exact dates.
