What Is the KAUST Global Fellowship?

The KAUST Global Fellowship Program is a merit-based postdoctoral fellowship designed to attract outstanding early-career researchers from around the world to King Abdullah University of Science and Technology in Thuwal, Saudi Arabia. Unlike traditional postdoctoral positions that place researchers into existing projects, this fellowship allows successful candidates to design and lead their own independent research project with the guidance of a KAUST faculty mentor.

Fellows are not simply lab assistants. They function as independent principal investigators within KAUST’s research ecosystem, with full access to the university’s core laboratories, supercomputing facilities, and multidisciplinary research platforms. The program runs for three years, with a formal review at the end of the second year to confirm continued funding for the final year.

The fellowship is closely aligned with Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030, which prioritises research and innovation as drivers of economic diversification and knowledge-based development. Each cohort typically selects no more than ten fellows, making the program highly competitive.

About King Abdullah University of Science and Technology

Founded in 2009 on the eastern shore of the Red Sea, KAUST has grown into the most acclaimed postgraduate research university in the Middle East. The university operates as a graduate-only institution, meaning all enrolled students and researchers hold at least a bachelor’s degree, and the academic environment is built around research intensity from day one.

KAUST consistently ranks among the world’s fastest-rising research universities. Its faculty includes top-tier international researchers, and its laboratories are equipped with cutting-edge technologies across genomics, nanotechnology, imaging, materials science, marine biology, and high-performance computing. The campus itself spans more than 36 square kilometres and includes a fully integrated research park, a marine research station on the Red Sea, and dedicated facilities for each academic division.

The university’s research mission is organised around four strategic priorities: Energy and Industrial Leadership, Sustainable Environment and Essential Needs, Health and Wellness, and Economies of the Future. These priorities guide investment decisions, faculty recruitment, and the types of research proposals the Global Fellowship Program seeks to attract.

Research Areas and Academic Divisions

The KAUST Global Fellowship supports research across the university’s three academic divisions. Applicants must align their proposed project with one of these divisions and identify a Host PI within it.

Biological and Environmental Science and Engineering (BESE)

BESE focuses on understanding biological systems, environmental processes, and their intersection with technology. Research areas include plant science, marine science, bioscience, bioengineering, environmental science and engineering, and Red Sea research. The division operates the Red Sea Research Center and the Desert Agriculture Initiative.

Computer, Electrical and Mathematical Science and Engineering (CEMSE)

CEMSE covers computer science, electrical and computer engineering, applied mathematics and computational science, and statistics. Research priorities include artificial intelligence, machine learning, cybersecurity, data science, robotics, high-performance computing, and numerical modelling. The division houses KAUST’s AI Initiative and the Extreme Computing Research Center.

Physical Science and Engineering (PSE)

PSE encompasses chemical engineering, chemistry, earth science and engineering, materials science and engineering, mechanical engineering, and applied physics. Research areas include catalysis, energy storage and conversion, water desalination and treatment, nanomaterials, advanced manufacturing, and carbon management.

KAUST also identifies cross-cutting priority areas that span multiple divisions: water, food, energy, health, artificial intelligence, and marine science with an emphasis on the Red Sea. Proposals that connect two or more divisions or address these cross-cutting themes are particularly encouraged.

Step-by-Step Application Process

The application process consists of two stages. Only applicants who successfully pass Stage 1 are invited to submit a full proposal in Stage 2.

Stage 1: Concept Note (1 September - 1 October 2026)

  1. Identify potential Host PIs. Use the KAUST Faculty Directory to find faculty members whose research aligns with your proposed work. You may identify up to two KAUST faculty members in the Stage 1 application. A Host PI letter is not required at this stage.
  2. Prepare your concept note. Download the official concept note template from the KAUST Global Fellowship website. The concept note should summarise your proposed research, its novelty, and its alignment with KAUST priorities.
  3. Register on the PlutoEdu platform. Create an account, complete the online application form, and upload your CV, two publications, and the concept note.
  4. Submit before the deadline. All materials must be submitted by 1 October 2026, 23:59 AST (Arabia Standard Time).

Stage 2: Full Proposal by Invitation (15 November - 15 January)

  1. Receive an invitation. Applicants whose concept notes pass the initial review will be invited to submit a full proposal. You will need to secure a confirmed Host PI and obtain their support letter.
  2. Prepare the full research proposal. Use the official Stage 2 template. The proposal must include detailed methodology, budget justification, timeline, and expected outcomes.
  3. Arrange reference letters. Two referees must upload their letters directly through the PlutoEdu platform before the deadline.
  4. Submit the full application. Upload the proposal, Host PI support letter, and any additional documents before 15 January 2027.

Selection Process and Evaluation Criteria

Applications are evaluated through a multi-stage process designed to identify the strongest candidates regardless of their country of origin or current institution.

  1. Eligibility check. All applications are verified against the program’s eligibility criteria. Incomplete applications or those submitted after the deadline are not considered.
  2. Internal division committee review. Each academic division (BESE, CEMSE, or PSE) reviews the proposals assigned to it and selects the strongest candidates to proceed.
  3. External peer review. Shortlisted proposals are sent to independent external reviewers who evaluate research excellence, innovation, and feasibility.
  4. Interviews. Final-stage candidates are interviewed, typically in May 2027. The interview panel includes senior KAUST faculty and program leadership.
  5. Final selection. The program office makes final decisions based on all review outcomes and the number of available fellowship slots.

The evaluation criteria used by reviewers include: research excellence and originality, innovation beyond the applicant’s existing work, feasibility of the proposed project, alignment with KAUST’s research priorities, and the quality of the Host PI relationship.

Research Proposal Tips

  • Demonstrate novelty. Your proposal must clearly show how the proposed research goes beyond your PhD or current work. Reviewers specifically look for independent thinking.
  • Align with KAUST priorities. Study KAUST’s four research priorities and the work of its academic divisions. A proposal that connects naturally to existing KAUST strengths will be more competitive.
  • Be specific about methodology. Vague proposals rarely succeed. Include clear research questions, hypotheses, experimental approaches, and expected outcomes.
  • Identify the right Host PI. Your Host PI is not just a letter writer. They will mentor you throughout the fellowship. Choose someone whose expertise genuinely matches your project and who has the resources to support your work.
  • Use the templates exactly. KAUST provides specific templates for the concept note and full proposal. Follow the formatting, page limits, and section structure precisely. Adherence to guidelines is itself evaluated.
  • Budget realistically. Research budgets vary by discipline. Study the official guidelines to understand what is allowable and prepare a budget that is both ambitious and justified.

How to Increase Your Chances of Selection

The KAUST Global Fellowship is highly competitive, with only about ten fellows selected per year. The following strategies can strengthen your application.

  • Start early. Identifying the right Host PI takes time. Begin researching KAUST faculty at least three to four months before the Stage 1 deadline. Reach out with a concise, professional email that includes your research background and why their lab is a good fit.
  • Attend the webinar. KAUST typically hosts a prospective applicant webinar in August. Attending gives you direct insight into what the selection committee values and allows you to ask clarifying questions.
  • Choose referees who know your work well. Generic reference letters do not strengthen an application. Select referees who can speak specifically about your research ability, independence, and potential as a future research leader.
  • Proofread everything. Errors in your application, especially in the research proposal, signal a lack of care. Ask a colleague to review your materials before submission.
  • Prepare for the interview. If invited to interview, practise presenting your research proposal clearly and concisely. Be ready to defend your methodology and explain why KAUST is the right place for your work.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Submitting a proposal that extends your PhD work. The fellowship explicitly requires novelty beyond your doctoral research. A proposal that reads like a continuation of your PhD will not succeed.
  • Contacting Host PIs too late. Faculty members receive many requests. Give them time to review your background and respond. A rushed request is unlikely to result in a strong support letter.
  • Ignoring the template. The concept note and full proposal templates exist for a reason. Applications that deviate from the required format may be rejected without review.
  • Missing the deadline. Late submissions are not accepted. Submit at least 48 hours before the deadline to avoid technical issues with the online platform.
  • Applying without understanding KAUST. A generic proposal that could be submitted to any university will not stand out. Demonstrate genuine knowledge of KAUST’s facilities, research strengths, and strategic priorities.
  • Submitting incomplete documents. Double-check that all required files have been uploaded correctly, that PDFs are readable, and that referees have submitted their letters on time.

Life at KAUST

KAUST’s campus is located in Thuwal, approximately 80 kilometres north of Jeddah on the Red Sea coast. The campus is a purpose-built research community designed to support both work and family life.

On-campus housing is provided free of charge to Global Fellows. The housing options range from apartments to villas depending on family size. The campus includes a supermarket, medical centre, sports facilities, restaurants, and recreational areas. The KAUST Schools provide education from early childhood through secondary school, with curricula based on international standards. Fellows with children receive schooling support for up to two children.

The research environment at KAUST is distinctly international. Over 120 nationalities are represented across the university community, and English is the primary working language. The university’s core laboratories are available to all researchers and include facilities for genomics, imaging, materials characterisation, nanofabrication, marine science, and high-performance computing.

Thuwal itself is a small coastal town, but Jeddah is easily accessible for shopping, dining, and cultural activities. KAUST organises regular community events, and the Red Sea coastline offers opportunities for diving, snorkelling, and marine research.

Questions and Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What is KAUST Global Fellowship?

The KAUST Global Fellowship is a three-year fully funded postdoctoral program at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology in Saudi Arabia. It supports early-career researchers who design and lead their own independent research project with mentorship from a KAUST faculty member.

Is KAUST Global Fellowship fully funded?

Yes. The fellowship includes a USD 75,000 annual living stipend, a research budget of up to USD 40,000 per year, free on-campus housing, comprehensive medical insurance, schooling for up to two children, and relocation and travel allowances.

Who is eligible to apply?

Applicants of any nationality who hold a PhD awarded within the last four years are eligible. You must also secure a KAUST faculty member as your Host PI before applying. KAUST alumni who have been affiliated with the university for more than 12 months are not eligible.

Can international students apply?

Yes. The fellowship is open to applicants from all countries. Selected fellows must meet Saudi Arabia’s residency and work permit requirements to obtain a visa.

What research fields are available?

Research proposals must align with one of three academic divisions: Biological and Environmental Science and Engineering, Computer, Electrical and Mathematical Science and Engineering, or Physical Science and Engineering. Cross-cutting priorities include water, food, energy, health, artificial intelligence, and marine science.

How much is the stipend?

The annual living stipend is USD 75,000. This is supplemented by a research budget of up to USD 40,000 per year, free housing, and comprehensive benefits.

How long is the fellowship?

The fellowship duration is three years. Progress is formally reviewed at the end of the second year to confirm continued funding for the third year.

What documents are required?

You need a CV (max 4 pages), two selected publications, a concept note or full research proposal using the official KAUST template, a Host PI support letter, and two reference letters uploaded directly by referees.

Is IELTS required?

The application form includes a self-assessment of English competency. Official language test scores are not listed as a mandatory document, but strong English skills are essential for conducting research and communicating at KAUST. Check the official guidelines for the most current language requirements.

Can I choose my supervisor?

Yes, but the program refers to them as a Host PI rather than a supervisor. You must identify a KAUST faculty member whose research aligns with your proposed work and secure their agreement to serve as your Host PI. You may name up to two potential Host PIs in the Stage 1 application.

When are results announced?

Final decisions and fellowship nominations are typically announced in June 2027 for the current cycle. Nominated fellows are expected to join KAUST within three to six months of the nomination.

Can I bring my family?

Yes. The fellowship includes comprehensive medical insurance for the fellow and their family, free housing suitable for families, and schooling support for up to two children at KAUST Schools.

What is the acceptance rate for the KAUST Global Fellowship?

The KAUST Global Fellowship is highly competitive with an estimated acceptance rate of 10-15%. The program receives hundreds of applications globally each cycle and typically selects approximately 10 fellows per cohort. Selection depends on research proposal quality, host PI fit, and publication record.

Is the USD 75,000 stipend taxable?

Yes, the stipend is paid as a salary and is tax-free in Saudi Arabia. The total compensation package exceeds USD 120,000 per year when including free housing, medical insurance, schooling, relocation, and travel benefits.

Do I need to contact a professor before applying?

Yes. A Host PI support letter from a KAUST faculty member is mandatory. You should contact potential PIs well before the deadline. The Host PI must co-fund 50% of the stipend and provide lab space and mentoring.
