What Is the UNICEF Internship Programme?

The UNICEF Internship Programme offers students and recent graduates the opportunity to gain practical work experience in one of the world’s largest and most respected international organisations. Interns work alongside UNICEF professionals on projects that directly contribute to the organisation’s mission of protecting children’s rights and improving the lives of the world’s most vulnerable children and adolescents.

Unlike many UN internships that are unpaid, UNICEF provides a monthly stipend to help cover living expenses, along with a one-time contribution toward travel and visa costs where funding is available. Internships typically last between six and 26 weeks and can be full-time or part-time depending on the office and role.

UNICEF interns are placed in one of three types of offices: headquarters in New York, regional offices serving specific geographic areas, or country offices that implement UNICEF programmes directly in the field. Each placement offers a different perspective on how UNICEF operates, from global policy development to local programme delivery.

About UNICEF

The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) was established in 1946 to provide emergency food and healthcare to children in countries devastated by World War II. Today, UNICEF operates in over 190 countries and territories, making it one of the most geographically extensive UN agencies. The organisation works with governments, civil society, and other UN agencies to deliver programmes in education, health, nutrition, water and sanitation (WASH), child protection, and social inclusion.

UNICEF employs over 15,000 staff worldwide, including child protection specialists, health experts, educators, data analysts, communications professionals, supply chain specialists, and policy advisors. The agency is headquartered in New York City, with regional offices in Panama, Senegal, Jordan, Thailand, Nepal, and Switzerland, plus country offices in virtually every developing nation.

For internship applicants, UNICEF’s scale means there are opportunities across virtually every professional field. An economics student might work on social policy analysis, a communications student could support advocacy campaigns, and a public health student might contribute to immunisation programme data. The key is matching your skills and interests to the specific vacancy that aligns with them.

Internship Locations

UNICEF offers internship placements in three categories of offices, each providing a distinct experience.

Headquarters (New York, USA). Interns at UNICEF headquarters work on global policy, advocacy, communications, and corporate functions. This is ideal for candidates interested in international policy, programme design, and the strategic direction of UNICEF’s work.

Regional offices. UNICEF has seven regional offices covering Latin America and the Caribbean (Panama), West and Central Africa (Senegal), Eastern and Southern Africa (Kenya), Middle East and North Africa (Jordan), Europe and Central Asia (Switzerland), South Asia (Nepal), and East Asia and the Pacific (Thailand). Regional office interns work on region-wide strategies, knowledge management, and technical support to country offices.

Country offices. Most UNICEF internships are in country offices, where interns see the direct impact of UNICEF’s programmes. Country office placements are available in over 150 countries and offer hands-on experience in programme implementation, monitoring, and community engagement.

How to Apply Step by Step

Step 1: Create a UNICEF Careers Profile

Visit the UNICEF careers portal and create an account. Complete your profile with accurate education and work experience details. Upload an updated CV and save a draft cover letter that you can customise for each application.

Step 2: Search for Internship Vacancies

Browse the internship vacancies on the UNICEF careers portal. Use filters to narrow by location, field, or type of internship. Read each vacancy notice carefully to understand the requirements, responsibilities, and application instructions.

Step 3: Tailor Your Application

Customise your cover letter for each vacancy. Address the specific skills, experience, and motivation the vacancy asks for. Reference UNICEF’s mission and explain how your background aligns with the office’s work.

Step 4: Submit Before the Deadline

Each vacancy has a specific closing date. Submit your application well before the deadline to avoid last-minute technical issues. Incomplete applications are not considered.

Step 5: Prepare for Interviews

If shortlisted, you will be invited for an interview, typically conducted via video call. Research UNICEF’s current priorities, review the vacancy details, and prepare examples of your relevant experience using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Step 6: Accept and Onboard

If selected, you will receive an internship offer letter. Complete the required onboarding steps, including medical clearance if needed, visa application, and travel arrangements.

Selection Process and Criteria

UNICEF selects interns through a competitive process managed by each hiring office. The exact process varies by vacancy but generally follows these stages.

  1. Application screening. HR reviews applications for completeness and eligibility against the minimum requirements.
  2. Technical review. The hiring team reviews applications against the desired qualifications and selects candidates for interviews.
  3. Interview. Shortlisted candidates participate in a competency-based interview, typically lasting 30-45 minutes. Questions assess motivation, relevant skills, teamwork, adaptability, and understanding of UNICEF’s work.
  4. Reference check. Selected candidates’ references are contacted to verify experience and suitability.
  5. Offer. The successful candidate receives an internship offer outlining the terms, stipend, and start date.

Tips for a Strong UNICEF Internship Application

  • Research the office. Each UNICEF office has a specific mandate. A cover letter written for the New York headquarters will not work for a country office in Kenya. Learn about the office’s current programmes and priorities before applying.
  • Connect to UNICEF’s mission. UNICEF is mission-driven. Demonstrating genuine commitment to children’s rights and development will strengthen your application significantly.
  • Highlight relevant skills. Focus on skills that match the vacancy, whether data analysis, programme management, communications, or research. Use specific examples with measurable results.
  • Be flexible on location. Country office internships offer the richest field experience but may be in challenging environments. Expressing willingness to work in different duty stations can expand your opportunities.
  • Apply early. UNICEF reviews applications on a rolling basis for most vacancies. Applying early in the window can increase your chances of being noticed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying without reading the vacancy carefully. If the vacancy asks for specific qualifications, your application must clearly demonstrate them. Generic applications are rejected quickly.
  • Submitting the same cover letter for every vacancy. Each UNICEF office and role is different. A tailored cover letter is essential.
  • Missing the deadline. UNICEF’s application system enforces deadlines strictly. Submitting even one minute late means your application will not be considered.
  • Ignoring language requirements. If the vacancy requires French or Spanish proficiency, provide evidence. Overstating your language ability is easily caught during the interview.
  • Forgetting proof of enrollment. Your application will be rejected if you cannot provide valid proof of enrollment or recent graduation.

Questions and Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Does UNICEF pay interns?

Yes. UNICEF provides a monthly stipend to help cover living expenses during the internship. The exact amount varies by duty station. UNICEF also offers a one-time lump sum contribution toward travel and visa costs subject to available funding.

How long is a UNICEF internship?

UNICEF internships typically last between 6 and 26 weeks, depending on the office and role. Both full-time and part-time arrangements are available. The specific duration is listed in each vacancy posting.

Can international students apply for UNICEF internships?

Yes. UNICEF accepts applicants from all nationalities. You must be enrolled in an accredited university programme or have graduated within the past two years. Language fluency in English, French, or Spanish is required.

What fields can I work in at UNICEF?

UNICEF hires interns across all its programme areas including education, health, nutrition, water and sanitation, child protection, social policy, communications, data analytics, supply chain, finance, and human resources.

Where are UNICEF interns placed?

Interns are placed at UNICEF headquarters in New York, seven regional offices worldwide, or country offices in over 150 countries. Country office placements offer the most direct field experience.

Do I need a specific degree to apply?

No specific degree is required. Your field of study should be relevant to the vacancy you are applying for. UNICEF values diversity and hires interns from all academic disciplines.

Can I apply if I graduated over two years ago?

No. UNICEF requires applicants to be currently enrolled in a degree programme or to have graduated within the past two years from the date of application.

What is the application process?

Applications are submitted through the UNICEF careers portal. You create a profile, search for internship vacancies, and apply directly to each posting with a tailored CV and cover letter. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed via video call.
