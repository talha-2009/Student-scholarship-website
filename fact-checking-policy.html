Fact Checking Policy

The checks OpportunityNest uses before publishing scholarship, internship, fellowship, competition, grant, and exchange listings.

Last updated: 2026-08-05 by Abdullah Ijaz Abbasi, Founder & Editor.

Pre-publication checks

Before publication, each opportunity is checked for provider identity, official application URL, deadline language, country or region, opportunity type, funding level, eligible applicants, and required documents where available. Listings with unclear or suspicious sources are excluded until the provider can be verified.

These checks are completed before a listing is marked live. Every field is confirmed against the provider's own materials rather than secondary aggregators, because third-party summaries frequently drift from what the provider actually says. If a detail cannot be confirmed from a primary source, we omit it or label it as unconfirmed rather than guessing.

What we verify on each listing

Each listing goes through the same field-by-field review. These are the areas we check most closely:

  • Provider identity — We confirm the organization or institution behind the opportunity by visiting its official domain, checking for a verifiable team or contact channel, and looking for a track record that matches the claimed scope. Scholarships from unknown domains or copied agency pages are not published.
  • Official application URL — The link in a listing must point to the provider's own application page, not to a broker, affiliate, or paid middleman. Every official URL is opened and checked for a working, on-topic page before publication.
  • Deadline language — We quote the deadline exactly as the provider states it, including time zones and local date formats, and we avoid translating deadlines into assumptions.
  • Country or region — The geographic scope (open to all nationalities, restricted to specific countries, or tied to study location) is recorded from the official eligibility text.
  • Opportunity type — We classify each listing as a scholarship, internship, fellowship, competition, grant, or exchange using the provider's own description so categories stay accurate for searching.
  • Funding level — Fully funded, partially funded, stipend, honorarium, paid, unpaid, and fee waiver are recorded exactly as described and are never promoted to a stronger label.
  • Eligible applicants — Age, level of study, nationality, field, and other eligibility rules are copied from the official source and kept in the provider's own wording.
  • Required documents — When a provider lists documents such as transcripts, essays, recommendation letters, or portfolios, we note them so applicants can prepare early.

Deadline and funding review

Deadlines are treated conservatively because missed dates harm applicants. If an official page uses rolling, annual, or variable deadlines, the listing states that clearly instead of inventing a date. Funding labels are also reviewed carefully: fully funded, partially funded, paid, unpaid, stipend, and fee waiver are not interchangeable.

We also flag deadlines that expire within two weeks of our review so applicants can judge whether applying is realistic. When a provider gives only an application window rather than a fixed date, we say so in plain language.

How we handle ambiguity

Some providers deliberately leave deadlines and funding open. We record that ambiguity instead of resolving it for them.

  • Rolling deadlines — If applications are accepted until positions are filled, the listing says "rolling" and points to the official page for the current status.
  • Annual deadlines — If a program recurs each year but has not announced the next cycle, the listing notes the typical timing and links to the official page rather than reusing an old date.
  • Variable deadlines — When dates differ by region, program, or intake, we list the condition and the official source for each case.
  • Unclear funding — If a provider does not state whether an opportunity is funded, we label the funding as "not specified" and do not imply paid support.

Human review

Automated scripts help structure pages and detect route issues, but editorial judgment is still required for source quality, wording, and applicant usefulness. Generated pages are reviewed for duplicate patterns, thin summaries, broken official links, missing metadata, and unclear application guidance.

Every page published through automation receives a human pass before it goes live. During this pass we read the listing as an applicant would, check that the most important facts appear near the top, and confirm that the application link is prominent and correct.

Fact-checking process step by step

Every listing follows the same pipeline from discovery to publication:

  • Discovery — Opportunities are identified from provider websites, official announcements, and submissions to the site.
  • Source verification — Each candidate listing is checked for a primary source we can cite and for provider identity.
  • Drafting from official information — The listing is written from the provider's own page, not from promotional summaries or aggregator copy.
  • Editorial review — A human editor checks every field, the application link, and the tone of the listing.
  • Publishing — Once verified, the listing is published and indexed for search.
  • Monitoring — Live listings are re-checked when we become aware of changes, reported errors, or broken links.

How to report an error

If you spot a mistake, an outdated deadline, or a broken link, tell us through the Contact page. Please include the exact listing URL, the field that looks wrong, what you believe it should say, and a link to the official source if you have one. We respond to correction requests and update verified errors promptly.

Updates due to provider changes

Providers change rules without notice, and we update listings when we learn about those changes. Our re-verification practices are described on the Verification Process page.

Limitations

Opportunity providers can change rules without notice. OpportunityNest summarizes public information and cannot guarantee admission, funding, visa approval, or selection outcomes. Applicants should always confirm final details on the official provider website.

This policy explains how we work, not a promise that every field is error-free. Our full editorial standards are published in the Editorial Policy, and the way we re-check live listings is covered in the Verification Process.
