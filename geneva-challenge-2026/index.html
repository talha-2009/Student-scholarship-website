Geneva Challenge 2026: Complete Guide to the Advancing Development Goals Contest

The Geneva Challenge is the Graduate Institute's flagship international competition for master's students. Teams of three to five students from at least two disciplines develop practical solutions to pressing global development problems for a share of the CHF 25,000 prize pool, with five finalist teams flown to Geneva to defend their ideas before an international jury.

What is the Geneva Challenge?

The Geneva Challenge, officially named the Advancing Development Goals Contest, is an annual international competition run by the Graduate Institute of International and Development Studies (the Graduate Institute) in Geneva, Switzerland. Launched in 2014 with the generous support of Ambassador Jenö Staehelin, the contest invites master's students from every region of the world to propose theoretically grounded and pragmatic solutions to a current international development problem.

The late Kofi Annan, former Secretary-General of the United Nations, served as the High Patron of the contest. Every year the competition builds on a single cross-cutting theme, and participating teams are deliberately required to work across academic disciplines so that each proposal benefits from genuinely different perspectives.

Quick facts

  • Host: Graduate Institute of International and Development Studies, Geneva, Switzerland
  • Level: Master's students (post-bachelor graduate programmes)
  • Team size: 3 to 5 students from at least 2 different disciplines
  • Total prize pool: CHF 25,000
  • Finalists: 5 teams, one per continent, travel and accommodation covered

Geneva Challenge 2026 theme and focus areas

The 2026 edition centered on the future of work. Teams were asked to design innovative and actionable solutions to structural labour market changes driven by technology, demographic shifts, climate change, and rising inequality. Proposals had to be:

  • Innovative - offering a genuinely new angle on an employment challenge
  • Interdisciplinary - integrating at least two disciplinary perspectives
  • Theoretically grounded - built on sound academic analysis
  • Practical and scalable - with clear implementation pathways and potential for wider adaptation

The contest favors actionable policy or programme solutions over purely academic analysis. Strong proposals clearly define a labour market problem, propose a feasible intervention, and demonstrate real potential for impact and scale. Typical angles included digital skills and AI and the workforce, youth employment, the gig economy, automation, reskilling and lifelong learning, and just transitions in developing economies.

Eligibility requirements

The Geneva Challenge has strict but simple eligibility rules. Confirm the current year's regulations on the official page before assembling a team.

  • Enrollment: All team members must be enrolled in a graduate programme at the time of registration, normally a post-bachelor or master's level programme of up to two years in duration.
  • Team composition: Teams of three to five graduate students. Individual participation is not admitted.
  • Interdisciplinary requirement: At least two different disciplinary perspectives must be represented within the team.
  • Proof of enrollment: Each member must provide an enrollment certificate or student card showing their name, graduate programme, and a recent issuance date.
  • Nationality: Open to students of all nationalities and universities worldwide.
  • Transitions: Students transitioning into graduate studies may register if they have already been accepted into a graduate programme at the time of registration.

How to apply step by step

  1. Assemble an interdisciplinary team of 3 to 5 master's students.
  2. Read the current year's call for proposals and regulations on the official website.
  3. Complete the registration form and send proof of enrollment for every team member before the registration deadline.
  4. Use the eleven-week preparation window to research, write, and refine your full written proposal.
  5. Submit the completed proposal before the submission deadline.
  6. If selected as a finalist, prepare a concise oral presentation and defense for the jury in Geneva.

Planning tip: The 2026 edition has closed. The next cycle's theme and deadlines are announced on the official Geneva Challenge page. Start team formation and topic research at least two months before registration opens.

How to write a winning proposal

Selection committees look for proposals that are both academically credible and practically actionable. Here is what a competitive Geneva Challenge submission looks like:

Define one sharp problem

Do not try to solve employment challenges in general. Narrow the proposal to a specific labour market problem, population, and context so the solution can be concrete and testable.

Bring real interdisciplinary depth

The two-discipline minimum is a floor, not a target. Teams that genuinely combine fields, for example economics with digital technology or public health with data science, tend to score higher than teams where one field dominates.

Ground the theory, then go practical

Anchor the analysis in credible evidence and literature, then spend most of the word count on the proposed intervention: what it is, how it works, who operates it, what it costs, and how it can scale.

Show feasibility and impact

Include an implementation pathway, realistic budget, stakeholders, and measurable outcomes. Judges reward proposals that could plausibly be piloted tomorrow.

Write clearly and format professionally

Follow the official template, keep paragraphs short, use headings, and proofread for clarity. A proposal the jury can read quickly is a proposal the jury can reward.

Geneva Challenge 2026 frequently asked questions

What is the Geneva Challenge 2026?

The Geneva Challenge 2026 is the annual Advancing Development Goals Contest organized by the Graduate Institute of International and Development Studies in Geneva, Switzerland. Teams of three to five master's students develop interdisciplinary proposals offering practical solutions to a pressing international development problem. The 2026 edition focused on the future of work and distributed CHF 25,000 in prizes.

Who can apply for the Geneva Challenge 2026?

Graduate students enrolled in a post-bachelor or master's level university programme at the time of registration. Participants must form teams of three to five students with at least two different disciplinary perspectives. Students of all nationalities and universities are eligible.

What is the theme of the Geneva Challenge 2026?

The 2026 edition centered on the future of work, addressing structural labour market transformations driven by technology, demography, climate change, and inequality. Teams proposed innovative, theoretically grounded, practical, and scalable solutions to employment challenges.

What are the Geneva Challenge 2026 prizes?

The contest distributes CHF 25,000 in prizes: CHF 10,000 for the winning project, CHF 5,000 for each of the two second-place teams, and CHF 2,500 for each of the two third-place teams.

Are travel costs covered for Geneva Challenge finalists?

Yes. Five finalist teams, one per continent, are invited to Geneva to present and defend their proposals before an international jury, with travel and accommodation fully covered.

Can I participate in the Geneva Challenge individually?

No. Participation is strictly team-based. Teams must consist of three to five master's-level students from at least two different academic disciplines.

What is the difference between the registration and submission deadlines?

The registration deadline is when teams complete the application form and send proof of enrollment. After registration, teams have about eleven weeks to prepare their full proposal, which is due by the submission deadline. For 2026 these were 20 May 2026 and 11 July 2026 respectively.

When was the Geneva Challenge 2026 deadline?

The registration deadline was 20 May 2026 at 23:59 CET and the submission deadline was 11 July 2026 at 23:59 CET.

How is the Geneva Challenge judged?

An interdisciplinary academic steering committee first selects three proposals per continent to publish on the competition website. An independent jury of experts with academic, governmental, and private sector backgrounds then reviews them and chooses the five finalist teams who present in Geneva.

Is the Geneva Challenge open to PhD students?

The contest targets graduate students enrolled in a post-bachelor or master's level programme of up to two years. Confirm the current year's regulations on the official page if you are a PhD student.

Is there an application fee for the Geneva Challenge?

No. Registration and participation are free. Teams only submit the registration form, proof of enrollment, and their written proposal.

When will the Geneva Challenge 2027 be announced?

The Geneva Challenge runs annually, and the next edition's details are typically announced in the winter or early spring of the preceding year. Follow the official Graduate Institute website for the 2027 call for proposals.
