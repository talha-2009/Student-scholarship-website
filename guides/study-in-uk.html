Study in the UK: Complete Guide for International Students

Last updated August 4, 2026 · Reviewed by Abdullah Ijaz Abbasi · 25 min read

The United Kingdom is home to some of the world's oldest and most prestigious universities, and four of the global top 10 sit here — Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, and UCL. More than 600,000 international students from over 200 countries study in the UK every year. With one-year master's programmes and a post-study Graduate Route, it offers a cost-effective path to a globally recognised education.

This 2026 guide covers everything you need to know: why the UK is worth it, the top universities, tuition and living costs with a full cost table, scholarships including Chevening and Commonwealth awards, admission and visa requirements, intakes, and a final checklist. Why Study in the UK?

The UK has one of the most respected higher education systems in the world, with teaching quality independently reviewed by the Quality Assurance Agency (QAA). At its centre sits the Russell Group, 24 research-intensive universities — including Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, UCL, LSE, Edinburgh, and King's College London — that produce around two-thirds of the research conducted at UK universities. For international students that means degrees recognised worldwide.

  • One-year master's degrees: Most taught master's run for 12 months instead of two, cutting tuition and living costs roughly in half compared with the US.
  • World-leading research: UK universities produce a disproportionate share of the world's most-cited research, and PhD students join some of the strongest groups in their field.
  • Work while you study: Student visa holders can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during holidays.
  • Post-study Graduate Route: Stay and work for two years after a bachelor's or master's, and three years after a PhD.
  • Multicultural campuses: Students from more than 200 countries study in the UK, and London hosts more international students than any other European city.

Did you know? UK institutions have produced more Nobel Prize winners than any other country except the United States.

Oxford and Cambridge follow the collegiate system: every student belongs to both a university and a college, with teaching in small tutorials and supervisions alongside lectures. Imperial is Britain's engineering and science powerhouse, LSE the world's leading social science institution, and UCL, Edinburgh, and King's combine long histories with huge research output. Degrees here carry global weight — but these are not the only strong universities in the country.

Choosing a university: Do not choose purely on ranking. Compare fees, scholarships, location, and whether the specific course is offered — a strong department at a less famous university can serve you better than a famous brand in a weak subject.

Cost of Studying in the UK

Tuition for international students ranges from about GBP 15,000 to GBP 40,000+ per year for master's programmes, with arts and humanities at the cheaper end and medicine, MBA, and lab-based science at the top. Living costs are the other major budget item: roughly GBP 10,000 to GBP 15,000 per year outside London and GBP 15,000 to GBP 20,000 inside the capital. The table below gives a breakdown by region.

By study level, undergraduate classroom courses run from about GBP 15,000 to 25,000 a year, lab-based and medical degrees from GBP 20,000 to 40,000+, taught master's from GBP 15,000 to 40,000+, MBAs from GBP 35,000 to 60,000+, and PhD tuition from GBP 15,000 to 30,000 — though most research students hold a funded studentship.

Remember the extras: the Immigration Health Surcharge (about GBP 776 per year) is paid with your visa, the visa fee is around GBP 500, and you should budget for flights and deposits. A one-year master's keeps these one-off costs to a single cycle.

Chevening is the UK Government's flagship scholarship: a fully funded one-year master's at any UK university, requiring two years of work experience and a November deadline — see our Chevening Scholarship page. Commonwealth Scholarships fund students from developing Commonwealth countries — research applicants should see the Commonwealth PhD Scholarship. The Gates Cambridge Scholarship and the Rhodes Scholarship fully fund study at Cambridge and Oxford respectively, and the Clarendon Fund supports 130+ graduate researchers each year.

Beyond these big names, most universities run their own international merit awards — tuition reductions of 10% to 50% that are often awarded automatically on application. Best strategy: Apply for Chevening and Commonwealth first — they are the most competitive and close early. At the same time, apply for automatic merit awards; a 20% tuition reduction on a one-year master's is a very real sum.

Admission Requirements

Requirements vary by university and programme, but the general expectations for international applicants are below.

Undergraduate applications through UCAS

Undergraduate applications go through UCAS. You choose up to five courses in one application and submit a single personal statement plus a school reference. Oxford and Cambridge close on 15 October; most other universities have a mid-January deadline. Many courses also require admissions tests — LNAT for law, UCAT for medicine, TMUA for maths and economics — so check early.

Postgraduate requirements

  • Master's: A bachelor's degree with at least an upper second class (2:1) honours equivalent — roughly a 3.2–3.5 GPA on a 4.0 scale or a 60–70% aggregate. Some competitive programmes ask for a first-class degree, and many MBAs require work experience.
  • PhD / MRes: A strong master's with a substantial research component, a detailed research proposal, and often a supervisor who has agreed to take you on.

English language requirements

  • IELTS Academic: 6.5–7.5 overall with minimum scores in each band; medicine, law, and journalism often ask for 7.5.
  • Alternatives: TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and Cambridge English are widely accepted, and some universities accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate. See our guide to scholarships without IELTS.

Required documents

  • Official academic transcripts and degree certificates
  • English proficiency results (or MOI certificate)
  • Personal statement and two academic references — see our personal statement guide and university admissions guide
  • Research proposal and CV for research degrees
  • Valid passport copy

Don't underestimate the 2:1: If your bachelor's GPA sits below that level, look for universities that accept a 2:2 with work experience, or consider a pre-master's pathway year.

Student Visa (CAS, IHS, Graduate Route)

International students need a Student visa, which replaced the old Tier 4 visa. You cannot apply until your university issues a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS), a unique reference number confirming your place. You then prove you can cover your first-year tuition and living costs, pay the Immigration Health Surcharge, and apply online. The steps:

  1. Receive your CAS: Accept your unconditional offer, pay any required deposit, and your university issues your CAS.
  2. Show financial proof: Hold your first-year tuition plus living costs — roughly GBP 1,334 per month in London and GBP 1,023 per month outside London — for 28 consecutive days before you apply.
  3. Pay the Immigration Health Surcharge: About GBP 776 per year of your visa, giving you access to the National Health Service during your stay.
  4. Submit the online application: Pay the fee (around GBP 500 outside the UK), book biometrics, and provide a tuberculosis test result if required for your country.
  5. Receive your decision: Most applications outside the UK are decided within about three weeks.

The Student visa allows you to work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during holidays, and on-course placements are allowed where they form part of your programme. Since January 2024, only postgraduates on research-based courses can bring dependants.

After your degree, the Graduate Route lets you stay and work — or look for work — for two years after a bachelor's or master's and three years after a PhD. There is no salary threshold or sponsorship requirement. See our complete student visa guide.

Apply early: You can apply for a Student visa up to six months before your course starts (and enter the UK up to one month before). Never book non-refundable flights until your visa is granted.

Intakes and Application Timeline

September is the main intake for UK universities, with most courses starting in late September. Many universities also offer a January intake, mainly for one-year master's programmes in business, computing, and engineering.

  • September / October: The main intake for nearly all courses.
  • January: A secondary intake at many universities, mainly for taught master's courses.

Recommended timeline

  • 12 months before: Shortlist universities; start IELTS preparation; note scholarship deadlines.
  • 9 months before: Take English tests; apply to universities; submit scholarship applications (Chevening closes in November).
  • 6 months before: Accept your offer, receive your CAS, apply for the Student visa, and pay the IHS.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much does it cost to study in the UK per year?

The total annual cost including tuition and living expenses is roughly GBP 30,000 to 55,000. Tuition ranges from GBP 15,000 to 40,000+ per year depending on the course, and living costs are about GBP 10,000 to 15,000 outside London and GBP 15,000 to 20,000 in London.

What IELTS score do I need to study in the UK?

Most universities require IELTS Academic 6.5 to 7.5 overall for postgraduate study, with minimum scores in each band. Courses in medicine, law, and journalism often ask for 7.0 to 7.5, while many undergraduate programmes accept 6.0 to 6.5.

Can I study in the UK without IELTS?

Yes, in many cases. Universities widely accept TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and Cambridge English in place of IELTS, and some accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate if your previous degree was taught in English. Always confirm with your chosen university first.

Can international students work in the UK while studying?

Yes. Student visa holders can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during holidays. Course-mandated work placements are also allowed.

How do I apply for a UK student visa?

Once your university issues a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS), you prove you can cover first-year tuition and living costs, pay the Immigration Health Surcharge, and apply online with biometrics. Most applications outside the UK are decided within about three weeks.

What is the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)?

The IHS is a fee you pay as part of your visa application that gives you access to the UK's National Health Service during your stay. It costs around GBP 776 per year of your visa.

What scholarships are available for international students in the UK?

The major fully funded awards are the Chevening Scholarship, Commonwealth Scholarships, Gates Cambridge, and the Rhodes Scholarship. The Clarendon Fund covers graduate research at Oxford, GREAT Scholarships provide about GBP 10,000 toward tuition, and most universities run their own merit-based awards.

How long can I stay in the UK after graduation?

Under the Graduate Route you can stay and work, or look for work, for two years after a bachelor's or master's degree, and three years after a PhD. There is no salary threshold or sponsor requirement.

Do I need a research proposal for a PhD in the UK?

Yes. PhD applications in the UK almost always require a detailed research proposal, and you are strongly advised to contact a potential supervisor before applying. Many departments also ask for a master's degree with a substantial research component.

What are the intake periods for UK universities?

September is the main intake for nearly all courses. Many universities also offer a January intake, mainly for taught master's programmes in business, computing, and engineering.

Study in the UK Checklist

  • Have I researched universities and compared scholarships, fees, and living costs?
  • Have I checked scholarship deadlines (Chevening closes in November)?
  • Have I taken an English proficiency test (IELTS 6.5–7.5 or equivalent)?
  • Have I prepared my personal statement and secured my references?
  • Have I submitted my UCAS (undergraduate) or direct (postgraduate) application?
  • Have I received my CAS and confirmed my course start date?
  • Have I prepared proof of funds, paid the IHS, and applied for my Student visa?
  • Have I arranged accommodation and budgeted for arrival costs?
