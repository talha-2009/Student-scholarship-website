Study in Australia (2026 Complete Guide): Universities, Scholarships, Visa, Costs & Requirements

Last updated August 4, 2026 · Reviewed by Abdullah Ijaz Abbasi · 30 min read

Australia is one of the world's most popular study destinations, attracting more than 600,000 international students each year. It combines a globally respected education system, six universities ranked in the world's top 100, a safe and multicultural society, and some of the most generous post-study work rights anywhere. For Pakistani, South Asian, and other international students, Australia remains a top choice because degrees are recognised worldwide, part-time work is allowed during study, and there are clear pathways to permanent residency after graduation.

This complete 2026 guide covers everything you need to know about studying in Australia — why the country is worth considering, the best universities, the exact costs and a full cost table, government and university scholarships, step-by-step Subclass 500 visa instructions, how to study after 12th grade, how to apply without IELTS, the best courses to choose, work rights, and permanent residency pathways. Why Study in Australia?

Australia consistently ranks among the top three English-speaking study destinations in the world, and the reasons are clear. The Australian higher education system is regulated by the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) and every course offered to international students must be registered with CRICOS (the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students). This means quality is audited, student protections are enforced, and your degree is recognised by employers and universities globally.

  • World-Class Universities: Six Australian universities rank inside the world's top 100 — the University of Melbourne, the Australian National University (ANU), the University of Sydney, UNSW Sydney, Monash University, and the University of Queensland.
  • Post-Study Work Rights: After graduation you can stay and work for 2 to 4 years on the Temporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485), and many graduates use that experience to qualify for permanent residency.
  • Work While Studying: International students can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during the semester and unlimited hours during scheduled holidays.
  • Safety and Quality of Life: Australia has one of the lowest crime rates among developed nations, and cities like Melbourne, Sydney, Adelaide, and Perth regularly rank among the most livable in the world.
  • Generous Scholarships: The Australian Government, through Australia Awards and the Research Training Program, plus individual universities, fund thousands of international students every year.
  • Multicultural Society: Nearly a third of Australia's population was born overseas, and more than 200 languages are spoken, so international students find a genuinely welcoming environment.

Did you know? Australia is a global education leader in research. ANU, Melbourne, and UNSW are all members of the Group of Eight, a coalition of Australia's most research-intensive universities that produces most of the country's academic output.

Study in Australia for Pakistani Students

Australia is a leading destination for Pakistani students, and the number of Pakistani enrolments has grown steadily over the past decade. There are several reasons: degrees are recognised by the HEC (Higher Education Commission) of Pakistan, the English-medium education system in Pakistan translates well to Australian entry requirements, and there are genuine scholarship and work pathways for Pakistani applicants.

Why Australian universities accept Pakistani students

  • English-medium education: Many Pakistani students qualify for an MOI (Medium of Instruction) waiver, which replaces IELTS for admission purposes.
  • Shared academic calendar: Pakistani A-Level, FSc, and intermediate students can apply directly to undergraduate programmes; bachelor's degree holders can apply for master's.
  • HEC recognition: Degrees from TEQSA-accredited Australian universities are recognised for jobs and further study in Pakistan.
  • Scholarship access: Pakistan is an eligible country for Australia Awards Scholarships, the Research Training Program, and university-specific awards.

What Pakistani students need to prepare

For admission, you generally need your academic transcripts, an English proficiency proof (IELTS/PTE/TOEFL or MOI certificate), a statement of purpose, and two to three reference letters. For the visa, you must show genuine student status, enough funds to cover tuition and living costs for one year, and evidence that you intend to return or progress legitimately to skilled migration. See our step-by-step guide on how to write a statement of purpose and our guide to student visa applications.

Tip for Pakistani applicants: Start your English test and document attestation (HEC and IBCC attestation for degrees and transcripts) early. Attestation in Pakistan can take several weeks, and Australian universities and the visa office expect attested, official copies.

Study in Australia After 12th Grade

Yes, you can study in Australia immediately after finishing 12th grade (FSc / intermediate / A-Levels). You have three main pathways depending on your grades, English score, and budget:

  1. Direct undergraduate entry: If you meet the ATAR-equivalent or year-12 percentage requirement (usually 60-75% or above depending on the university) and the English requirement, you can apply straight to a three-year bachelor's degree.
  2. Foundation programme: If your grades fall slightly below direct entry, most universities offer a one-year foundation programme that guarantees progression to year one of the degree.
  3. Vocational (VET) pathway: Diploma-level courses at TAFE or private colleges are cheaper and shorter, and many articulate into the second year of a university bachelor's degree. This is a popular, budget-friendly route.

Documents needed after 12th

  • Matriculation and intermediate (FSc / A-Level) certificates and transcripts
  • English proficiency: IELTS 6.0-6.5, or PTE/TOEFL equivalent, or an MOI certificate from an English-medium institution
  • Valid passport and, for most applicants, evidence of financial capacity
  • For A-Level students: the equivalence certificate from IBCC

Undergraduate applicants can also explore undergraduate scholarships, many of which are open to students entering straight after 12th grade.

Study in Australia Without IELTS

Yes, it is possible to study in Australia without IELTS. Universities accept several alternatives, and the important distinction is between the university admission requirement and the visa requirement. The visa office generally accepts the same evidence as universities, but you should confirm both.

Accepted alternatives to IELTS

  • Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate: If your previous degree or schooling was taught entirely in English, your institution can issue an MOI letter. Pakistani universities such as NUST, LUMS, IBA Karachi, Aga Khan University, and FAST routinely issue these, and Australian universities widely accept them.
  • PTE Academic: Fully accepted across all Australian universities and for the student visa. Equivalent bands are typically 42-58.
  • TOEFL iBT: Accepted almost everywhere; typical requirements are 79-100.
  • Duolingo English Test: An increasing number of universities accept Duolingo, although it is not accepted for the visa itself by Home Affairs.
  • English pathway (ELICOS): If you fall slightly short, universities offer packaged English language courses that lead directly into your degree without a separate IELTS test.
  • University's own English test: Some universities offer their own English proficiency assessments or interviews.

Important: An MOI waiver is never automatic. Check the specific university and course page to confirm they accept MOI, and keep your English certificate ready in case the visa office asks for it. For the full list of no-IELTS options, see our guide to Australia scholarships without IELTS.

The official living-cost figure used by the Department of Home Affairs for 2025-26 is approximately AUD 29,710 per year for a single student (this figure is updated periodically, so always check the current requirement).

  • On-campus housing: Convenient and community-focused, but usually the most expensive option.

Best Courses to Study in Australia

Choosing the right course matters for cost, employability, and post-study work. Australia has world-leading strengths in several fields, and studying in a shortage occupation can improve your skilled-migration prospects.

  • Engineering (civil, mechanical, electrical, mining): Australian mining and infrastructure engineering degrees are world-class, and engineers are consistently on the skilled occupation list.
  • Information Technology and Data Science: Computer science, cybersecurity, and data analytics graduates are in high demand across the country and for PR points.
  • Health sciences and Nursing: Nursing and allied health are on the critical skills shortage list, making these among the most employable degrees for PR pathways.
  • Business and Accounting: MBA and accounting programs offer strong career outcomes, especially at UNSW, Melbourne, and Monash.
  • Education and Teaching: Teachers are in short supply in Australia; a teaching degree combined with the right skills assessment can lead to PR.
  • Environmental and Agricultural Science: UQ and UWA lead globally in these fields, which align with Australia's green-energy priorities.
  • Architecture and Design: RMIT and UTS are internationally ranked for creative and design programs.

PR planning: If permanent residency is your goal, choose a course on the current skilled occupation list and check the state-nominated occupation lists too, because state nomination (Subclass 190/491) can add 15 points. Course choice affects your points more than most students realise.

Admission Requirements

Requirements vary by university and program, but the following are the general expectations for international applicants.

Academic requirements

  • Undergraduate: Secondary education equivalent to Australian Year 12, typically with 60-75% or an ATAR-equivalent grade. A-Level and IB students need the relevant subject combinations.
  • Master's: A relevant bachelor's degree with a minimum GPA (usually 2.5-3.0 on a 4.0 scale, or a 60-70% aggregate). Some programs, especially MBA, require work experience.
  • PhD: A relevant master's degree with a research component, a research proposal, and usually a supervisor match before you apply.

English language requirements

  • IELTS Academic: 6.0-7.5 overall (minimum 6.0 in each band) depending on the program; teaching, medicine, and law ask for higher scores.
  • TOEFL iBT: 79-100 overall.
  • PTE Academic: 50-65 overall, accepted everywhere.
  • Cambridge (CAE/CPE): 169-185 overall.

Required documents

  • Certified academic transcripts and certificates
  • English proficiency results (or MOI certificate)
  • Statement of Purpose — use our SOP writing guide
  • Two to three reference letters — see our recommendation letter guide
  • Updated CV/resume for graduate programs
  • Portfolio for design, architecture, and creative programs
  • Valid passport copy

Australia Student Visa (Subclass 500) — Step by Step

The Student Visa (Subclass 500) lets you study full-time at a CRICOS-registered institution in Australia. The application is done online through ImmiAccount on the Department of Home Affairs website. Follow this step-by-step process:

  1. Receive your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE): You must first be accepted into a CRICOS-registered course and pay the required deposit. The university issues your CoE, which is the single most important document for the visa.
  2. Check your eligibility: You must meet the Genuine Student (GS) requirement, English proficiency, financial capacity, health, and character requirements.
  3. Gather your documents: Passport, CoE, proof of funds (tuition + living costs + travel), OSHC, English test results (or evidence of exemption), GS statement, and police clearance certificates.
  4. Arrange health insurance (OSHC): Overseas Student Health Cover is mandatory for the whole visa period. You can buy it from providers like BUPA, Medibank, nib, or Allianz.
  5. Complete a health examination: Book the medical exam with a panel physician approved by Home Affairs if required for your country or visa length.
  6. Submit the application online: Create an ImmiAccount, complete Form 157A, attach all documents, and pay the visa application charge (approximately AUD 1,600 for most applications, 2025-26).
  7. Wait for processing: Processing usually takes 4 to 12 weeks, but can be faster or slower depending on your country and application. Do not book flights until the visa is granted.
  8. Receive your visa and check conditions: Check your visa grant letter for work conditions (48 hours per fortnight) and your visa expiry date.

Fast-track tip: Apply as early as possible after receiving your CoE. Lodging the visa 3 to 6 months before your course start date gives you time for health checks, biometrics, and any additional document requests. See our complete student visa guide for more detail.

Genuine Student (GS) requirement

Since March 2024, the old GTE (Genuine Temporary Entrant) statement has been replaced by the Genuine Student requirement. You need to demonstrate that you are a genuine student who understands the course and its costs, and who will comply with visa conditions. Be honest, specific, and prepared to answer questions in a short written statement.

Working While Studying in Australia

Working part-time is one of the biggest financial advantages of studying in Australia. Rules and wages are set by the Fair Work Ombudsman and apply to all workers, including international students.

  • Hours during the semester: Up to 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session.
  • Hours during breaks: Unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks and holidays.
  • Minimum wage: Approximately AUD 24 per hour (2025-26 national minimum), though casual roles in hospitality and retail usually pay more with loadings.
  • Dependants: If you are studying a master's or PhD, your spouse can work unlimited hours; if you are an undergraduate, they are capped at 48 hours per fortnight.

Popular part-time jobs for students

  • Hospitality: barista, waiter, kitchen hand, café assistant
  • Retail: sales assistant, cashier, stock hand
  • On-campus: library assistant, research assistant, student ambassador
  • Delivery and rideshare: food delivery and courier work
  • Tutoring: private tuition in your strongest subjects
  • Administrative: reception, data entry, office support

Working 15 to 20 hours a week at the minimum wage can cover a meaningful share of your living costs, but do not let work damage your studies — maintaining good grades protects your visa, scholarships, and post-study options. Post-Study Work Rights and Permanent Residency

Australia's post-study work rights are among the most generous in the world, and many students use their degree as a genuine pathway to permanent residency.

Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

This visa lets you live, study, and work after graduation. Duration depends on your qualification:

QualificationWork Rights
Bachelor's degree2 – 4 years
Master's by coursework2 – 3 years
Master's by research3 – 4 years
PhDUp to 4 years
Regional study bonusAdditional 1 – 2 years for graduates who studied outside major cities

Permanent residency pathways

  • Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189): Points-based; no sponsorship needed, but highly competitive.
  • Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190): Requires nomination by a state or territory government; adds 5 points.
  • Skilled Work Regional Visa (Subclass 491): For regional areas; can convert to PR after 3 years.
  • Employer-sponsored visas (Subclass 482 to 186): After working for an employer, they can sponsor you for PR.

Planning ahead: PR is a long game. Choose a shortage occupation, study outside a major city where possible, improve your English score, and keep your skills assessment documents ready. These choices collectively add points and open more nomination options.

Intake Periods and Application Timeline

Australian universities mostly run two intakes, with some programs offering a third trimester intake.

  • Semester 1 (February): The main intake for most programs. Apply between June and November of the previous year.
  • Semester 2 (July): Secondary intake for many programs. Apply between January and April.
  • Trimester / summer intakes: Some universities and business schools offer intakes in March or November for specific programs.

Recommended timeline

  • 12-18 months before: Shortlist universities and courses; start IELTS/PTE preparation; research scholarships.
  • 9-12 months before: Take English tests; gather transcripts; apply to universities; apply for government scholarships (Australia Awards close early).
  • 6-9 months before: Receive offers; accept and pay deposit; get your CoE; arrange OSHC.
  • 3-6 months before: Apply for the Subclass 500 visa; book flights and accommodation; arrange pre-departure checks.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much does it cost to study in Australia per year?

The total annual cost including tuition and living expenses is roughly AUD 45,000 to 75,000. Tuition ranges from AUD 25,000 to 50,000 per year depending on the programme, and living costs range from about AUD 22,000 to 30,000 per year depending on the city.

Can I study in Australia without IELTS?

Yes. Many universities accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate if your previous education was taught in English, or alternative tests such as PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, and the Duolingo English Test. English pathway programmes (ELICOS) are another route.

What is the minimum IELTS score required to study in Australia?

For the Subclass 500 student visa the minimum is generally IELTS 5.5 (or equivalent). Australian universities typically require 6.0 to 6.5 for undergraduate programmes and 6.5 to 7.5 for postgraduate programmes; medicine, law, and teaching often need 7.0 to 8.0.

How do I apply for an Australia student visa (Subclass 500)?

Apply online through the Department of Home Affairs (ImmiAccount) using your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), proof of funds, English proficiency evidence, Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), a Genuine Student statement, and health and character clearances. Processing typically takes 4 to 12 weeks.

Can international students work in Australia while studying?

Yes. Students on a Subclass 500 visa can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during study periods and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. The national minimum wage is about AUD 24 per hour (2025-26).

What scholarships are available for international students in Australia?

Australia Awards scholarships are fully funded by the Australian Government, the Research Training Program (RTP) covers tuition and a stipend for research degrees, the Destination Australia Scholarship pays AUD 15,000 per year for regional study, and most universities offer their own merit scholarships such as the ANU Chancellor's and Sydney Scholars awards.

What are the post-study work rights in Australia?

Graduates of CRICOS-registered courses can apply for the Temporary Graduate visa (Subclass 485), which offers 2 to 4 years of work rights depending on your qualification and study location. PhD graduates can stay for up to 4 years.

Can I get permanent residency after studying in Australia?

Yes. After gaining work experience on a Subclass 485 visa, many graduates move to permanent residency through the Skilled Independent (189), Skilled Nominated (190), Skilled Work Regional (491), or employer-sponsored (482 to 186) visas.

Can Pakistani students study in Australia?

Yes. Pakistani students are welcome at Australian universities and can apply for government scholarships such as Australia Awards and the Research Training Program, plus university-specific scholarships. Many institutions accept an MOI certificate from English-medium Pakistani universities in place of IELTS.

Can I study in Australia after 12th grade?

Yes. You can apply directly to undergraduate programmes after 12 years of schooling (equivalent to Australian Year 12). Options include foundation programmes if you do not yet meet direct entry, and vocational (VET) diploma pathways that can later articulate into a bachelor's degree.

What is OSHC and is it mandatory?

OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover) is compulsory health insurance for all international students on a Subclass 500 visa. It covers doctor visits, hospital care, and some prescriptions, and typically costs AUD 500 to 700 per year for single cover.

What are the intake periods for Australian universities?

Most universities offer two main intakes: Semester 1 in February and Semester 2 in July. Some also offer summer or trimester intakes in November or March. Apply 6 to 12 months ahead, and submit your visa application 3 to 6 months before your course starts.
