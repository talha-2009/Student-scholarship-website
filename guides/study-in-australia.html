Study in Australia: Complete Guide for International Students 2026

Last updated July 31, 2026 · 25 min read

Australia is one of the world's most popular study destinations, attracting over 500,000 international students annually. Known for its high-quality education system, vibrant multicultural cities, and excellent post-study work opportunities, Australia offers a unique combination of academic excellence and lifestyle benefits. With six of the world's top 100 universities and a strong focus on research and innovation, the Australian education system provides international students with globally recognized qualifications in a safe, welcoming environment.

This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about studying in Australia for international students, including top universities, scholarships, student visa requirements, costs, and post-study work opportunities.

Why Study in Australia?

Australia consistently ranks among the top three study destinations globally, and for good reason. The Australian education system is regulated by the Australian Government's Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA), ensuring all institutions meet rigorous quality standards. Here are the key benefits:

  • World-Class Universities: Six Australian universities rank in the world's top 100, including the University of Melbourne, Australian National University (ANU), University of Sydney, UNSW Sydney, University of Queensland, and Monash University.
  • Post-Study Work Rights: International students can work for 2-4 years after graduation depending on their degree level through the Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485).
  • Safe and Welcoming: Australia is one of the safest countries for international students, with low crime rates and a multicultural society that celebrates diversity.
  • Quality of Life: Australian cities consistently rank among the world's most livable, with Melbourne, Sydney, Adelaide, and Perth regularly appearing in global top 10 lists.
  • Generous Scholarships: The Australian Government and universities offer numerous fully funded and partially funded scholarships for international students.
  • Work While Studying: International students can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during semesters and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks.
  • CRICOS Registration: All institutions offering courses to international students must be registered on CRICOS (Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students), ensuring quality and compliance.

Best Universities in Australia for International Students

Australia is home to 43 universities, with 40 being public institutions. The following universities consistently rank among the best globally and are popular choices for international students:

  • University of Melbourne: Australia's top-ranked university (consistently in global top 20-30). Strong across all disciplines including medicine, law, engineering, and arts. Located in Melbourne, Victoria.
  • Australian National University (ANU): Located in Canberra, Australia's capital. Strong in research, policy, sciences, and international relations. Known for its research-intensive environment.
  • University of Sydney: One of Australia's oldest universities (founded 1850). Strong in law, medicine, business, and arts. Located in Sydney, New South Wales.
  • UNSW Sydney (University of New South Wales): Strong in engineering, business, law, and medicine. Known for industry connections and graduate employability. Located in Sydney.
  • University of Queensland (UQ): Strong in biological sciences, environmental studies, agriculture, and medicine. Located in Brisbane, Queensland.
  • Monash University: Strong in pharmacy, education, engineering, and business. Has campuses in Melbourne and internationally. Known for research impact.
  • University of Western Australia (UWA): Located in Perth. Strong in mining engineering, agriculture, and marine sciences.
  • University of Adelaide: Located in South Australia. Strong in engineering, computer science, and health sciences.

All Australian universities are CRICOS-registered, ensuring they meet the quality standards set by the Australian Government's Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA).

Cost of Studying in Australia for International Students

Understanding the costs is essential for planning your education in Australia. Here's a detailed breakdown:

Tuition Fees by Degree Level

  • Undergraduate Bachelor's Degree: AUD $25,000 - $45,000 per year
  • Master's Degree: AUD $30,000 - $50,000 per year
  • PhD/Doctoral Degree: AUD $20,000 - $45,000 per year (many PhD students receive scholarships)
  • Vocational Education and Training (VET): AUD $5,000 - $22,000 per year
  • English Language Courses: AUD $300 - $500 per week

Living Costs

The Australian Department of Home Affairs requires international students to demonstrate financial capacity of AUD $24,505 per year (as of 2024). However, actual living costs vary by city:

  • Sydney & Melbourne: AUD $25,000 - $35,000 per year (most expensive)
  • Brisbane, Perth, Adelaide: AUD $20,000 - $28,000 per year
  • Regional Areas: AUD $18,000 - $24,000 per year (more affordable)

Accommodation Costs

  • On-campus housing: AUD $150 - $350 per week
  • Private rental: AUD $200 - $500 per week
  • Shared accommodation: AUD $100 - $250 per week
  • Homestay: AUD $235 - $325 per week (includes meals)

Other Expenses

  • OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover): AUD $500 - $700 per year
  • Textbooks & supplies: AUD $500 - $1,000 per year
  • Transport: AUD $30 - $60 per week
  • Food & groceries: AUD $80 - $150 per week
  • Entertainment: AUD $50 - $100 per week

Scholarships in Australia for International Students

Australia offers numerous scholarship opportunities for international students at undergraduate, postgraduate, and PhD levels. Here are the major scholarship programs:

Government-Funded Scholarships

  • Australia Awards Scholarships: Fully funded scholarships for students from developing countries in Asia, Pacific, Africa, and the Middle East. Covers tuition, living expenses, airfare, and health insurance.
  • Research Training Programme (RTP) Scholarships: Government-funded scholarships for domestic and international research students (Master's by Research and PhD). Covers tuition offset and living stipend.
  • Destination Australia Scholarship: AUD $15,000 per year for students studying at regional campuses to encourage regional education.
  • Endeavour Leadership Program: Short-term scholarships for study, research, and professional development in Australia.

University-Specific Scholarships

  • Melbourne International Undergraduate Scholarship: 50% tuition fee waiver for high-achieving international students at University of Melbourne.
  • ANU Chancellor's International Scholarship: 25-50% tuition fee waiver at Australian National University.
  • Sydney Scholars Awards: AUD $10,000 per year for undergraduate students at University of Sydney.
  • UNSW International Scholarships: Various scholarships ranging from AUD $5,000 to full tuition waivers.
  • Monash International Merit Scholarship: Up to AUD $10,000 per year for high-achieving students.

Requirements to Study in Australia

Admission requirements vary by university and program, but here are the general requirements for international students:

Academic Requirements

  • Undergraduate Programs: Completion of secondary education (high school) equivalent to Australian Year 12. Minimum GPA requirements vary (typically 60-75% for most universities).
  • Master's Programs: A relevant bachelor's degree with minimum GPA (usually 2.5-3.0 on 4.0 scale or equivalent). Some programs require work experience.
  • PhD Programs: A relevant master's degree with research component. Research proposal and supervisor match required.

English Language Requirements

  • IELTS: Overall 6.0-7.5 (minimum 6.0 in each band) depending on program
  • TOEFL iBT: Overall 79-100 (minimum 18-22 in each section)
  • PTE Academic: Overall 50-65 (minimum 50 in each communicative skill)
  • Cambridge English (CAE/CPE): Overall 169-185

Some universities offer English language pathway programs if you don't meet the minimum requirements. See our comprehensive IELTS preparation guide for detailed information.

Required Documents

  • Academic transcripts: Certified copies of previous qualifications
  • English proficiency test scores: IELTS, TOEFL, or PTE results
  • Statement of Purpose (SOP): Explaining your study goals and reasons for choosing Australia
  • Letters of Recommendation: Usually 2-3 academic or professional references
  • Resume/CV: For graduate programs, especially MBA and research degrees
  • Portfolio: Required for design, architecture, and creative arts programs
  • Passport copy: Valid passport details

Study in Australia Without IELTS

Yes, it's possible to study in Australia without IELTS. Here are the alternatives:

  • English Medium Instruction: If your previous education was in English (e.g., from an English-speaking country or English-medium institution), many universities waive IELTS requirements.
  • University English Tests: Some universities accept their own English proficiency tests or interviews.
  • Duolingo English Test: Some universities accept Duolingo scores (check with individual institutions).
  • English Pathway Programs: If you don't meet English requirements, you can take English language courses (ELICOS) before your main program.
  • PTE Academic: Widely accepted alternative to IELTS across Australian universities.

Australia Student Visa (Subclass 500)

The Student Visa (Subclass 500) allows you to study full-time in Australia. Here's what you need to know:

Visa Requirements

  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE): Must be enrolled in a CRICOS-registered course
  • Proof of Financial Capacity: AUD $24,505 per year for living costs (plus tuition fees for first year)
  • English Proficiency: IELTS 5.5-6.0 minimum (varies by education sector)
  • OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover): Mandatory health insurance for the duration of your visa
  • GTE Statement: Genuine Temporary Entrant statement explaining your study intentions
  • Health Examination: Medical tests as required by the Department of Home Affairs
  • Character Requirements: Police clearance certificates

Work Rights on Student Visa

  • During Semester: Work up to 48 hours per fortnight
  • During Scheduled Breaks: Unlimited work hours
  • Family Members: Dependents can work unlimited hours if you are pursuing a master's or PhD

For detailed visa information, refer to our comprehensive Student Visa Guide.

Post-Study Work Visa and Permanent Residency

One of the major advantages of studying in Australia is the opportunity to work after graduation and potentially transition to permanent residency.

Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485)

This visa allows international graduates to live, study, and work in Australia temporarily after completing their studies. The duration depends on your qualification:

  • Bachelor's Degree: 2-4 years (depending on location of study)
  • Master's by Coursework: 2-3 years
  • Master's by Research: 3-4 years
  • PhD: 4 years
  • Regional Study Bonus: Additional 1-2 years if you studied in a regional area

Permanent Residency Pathways

After gaining work experience in Australia, you may be eligible for permanent residency through:

  • Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189): Points-based visa for skilled workers without employer sponsorship
  • Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190): Requires nomination by an Australian state or territory government
  • Skilled Work Regional (Subclass 491): For skilled workers nominated by regional areas
  • Employer-Sponsored Visas: Subclass 482 (Temporary) and Subclass 186 (Permanent)

Part-Time Jobs in Australia for International Students

Working while studying can help cover living expenses and gain valuable Australian work experience. Popular part-time jobs for international students include:

  • Retail: Sales assistant, cashier, stock handler
  • Hospitality: Waiter, barista, kitchen hand, customer service
  • Administrative: Office assistant, receptionist, data entry
  • Tutoring: Academic tutoring, language teaching
  • Delivery Services: Food delivery, package delivery
  • Campus Jobs: Library assistant, research assistant, campus tour guide

The minimum wage in Australia is approximately AUD $23.23 per hour (as of 2024), making part-time work financially rewarding.

Accommodation in Australia for International Students

Finding the right accommodation is crucial for your study experience. Here are the main options:

  • On-Campus Housing: Convenient, close to facilities, includes utilities. More expensive but offers community living.
  • Private Rental: More independence, can choose location. Requires lease commitment and bond payment.
  • Shared Accommodation: Most affordable option for students. Share rent and utilities with housemates.
  • Homestay: Live with an Australian family. Good for cultural immersion and improving English. Includes meals.
  • Purpose-Built Student Accommodation (PBSA): Modern facilities designed for students with amenities like gyms and study rooms.

Intake Periods in Australia

Australian universities typically have two main intakes:

  • Semester 1 (February Intake): Main intake for most programs. Application deadlines: October-December of previous year.
  • Semester 2 (July Intake): Secondary intake. Application deadlines: April-May of same year.
  • Summer/Trimester Intakes: Some universities offer additional intakes in November or March for specific programs.

Application Timeline and Process

Planning ahead is crucial for a smooth application process. Here's a recommended timeline:

12-18 Months Before Intake

  • Research universities and programs
  • Check admission requirements
  • Prepare for English proficiency tests (IELTS, TOEFL, PTE)
  • Start scholarship research

9-12 Months Before Intake

  • Take English proficiency tests
  • Gather required documents (transcripts, references, SOP)
  • Submit university applications
  • Apply for scholarships

6-9 Months Before Intake

  • Receive offer letters
  • Accept offer and pay deposit
  • Receive Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)
  • Arrange OSHC (health insurance)

3-6 Months Before Intake

  • Apply for Student Visa (Subclass 500)
  • Book flights and accommodation
  • Arrange pre-departure requirements
  • Attend pre-departure orientation

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying Late: Many students miss deadlines and rush applications, reducing chances of acceptance and scholarships.
  • Not Researching Scholarships: Thousands of scholarships go unclaimed because students don't apply.
  • Ignoring Regional Options: Regional universities offer lower costs, better scholarship opportunities, and extended post-study work rights.
  • Poor SOP Preparation: A generic statement of purpose can lead to rejection. Customize for each university.
  • Underestimating Costs: Many students budget only for tuition and forget living expenses, health insurance, and emergencies.
  • Not Checking Course Accreditation: Ensure your program is CRICOS-registered and recognized in your home country.
  • Visa Application Errors: Incomplete or incorrect visa applications can lead to delays or rejection.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can international students work in Australia while studying?

Yes, international students on a Subclass 500 visa can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during semesters and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. This helps cover living expenses and gain local work experience.

What is the minimum IELTS score required to study in Australia?

IELTS requirements vary by university and program. Generally, undergraduate programs require IELTS 6.0-6.5, while postgraduate programs require 6.5-7.5. Some programs like medicine and law may require higher scores (7.5-8.0).

How much does it cost to study in Australia per year?

The total annual cost including tuition and living expenses ranges from AUD $45,000 to $75,000. Tuition fees range from AUD $25,000-$50,000 per year, and living costs range from AUD $20,000-$27,000 per year depending on the city.

Can I study in Australia without IELTS?

Yes, you can study in Australia without IELTS if you have completed your previous education in English (English medium instruction), or by taking alternative tests like PTE Academic, TOEFL, or Duolingo. Some universities also offer English pathway programs.

What are the post-study work rights in Australia?

International graduates can apply for the Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485), which allows 2-4 years of work rights depending on your degree level. Bachelor's graduates get 2-4 years, master's graduates get 2-3 years, and PhD graduates get 4 years.

How do I apply for an Australia student visa?

To apply for a Subclass 500 student visa, you need: Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from a CRICOS-registered institution, proof of financial capacity (AUD $24,505/year), English proficiency test scores, OSHC health insurance, a GTE statement, and health/character clearances. Apply online through the Department of Home Affairs website.

What is OSHC and is it mandatory?

OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover) is mandatory health insurance for all international students in Australia. It covers medical and hospital care during your stay. Costs range from AUD $500-$700 per year depending on the provider.

Which are the best universities in Australia for international students?

The top Australian universities for international students include University of Melbourne, Australian National University (ANU), University of Sydney, UNSW Sydney, University of Queensland, Monash University, University of Western Australia, and University of Adelaide. All are CRICOS-registered and globally ranked.

What scholarships are available for international students in Australia?

Major scholarships include Australia Awards (fully funded), Research Training Programme (RTP) for research students, Destination Australia Scholarship for regional study, and university-specific scholarships like Melbourne International Scholarship, ANU Chancellor's Scholarship, and Sydney Scholars Award.

Can I get permanent residency after studying in Australia?

Yes, Australia offers pathways to permanent residency for international graduates. After gaining work experience on the Temporary Graduate Visa (Subclass 485), you may be eligible for skilled visas like Subclass 189, 190, or 491, or employer-sponsored visas like Subclass 186.

What are the intake periods for Australian universities?

Australian universities primarily have two intakes: Semester 1 (February) and Semester 2 (July). Some universities also offer summer or trimester intakes in November or March for specific programs. Apply 3-6 months before your intended intake.

How much can I earn working part-time in Australia?

The minimum wage in Australia is approximately AUD $23.23 per hour. International students typically earn AUD $20-$30 per hour in retail, hospitality, or administrative roles, which can help cover a significant portion of living expenses.

What documents are required for university application in Australia?

Required documents include academic transcripts, English proficiency test scores (IELTS/TOEFL/PTE), Statement of Purpose (SOP), letters of recommendation, resume/CV, passport copy, and portfolio (for creative programs). Some programs may require additional documents.

Is Australia safe for international students?

Australia is consistently ranked among the safest countries for international students. The country has low crime rates, strict safety regulations for education providers, and strong student support services. Australian cities also rank among the world's most livable.

What is the GTE statement for Australia student visa?

The GTE (Genuine Temporary Entrant) statement is a written explanation of your study intentions, why you chose Australia and the specific institution, and how the course benefits your career. It's a key requirement for the Subclass 500 student visa application.

Can my family accompany me to Australia on a student visa?

Yes, you can include your spouse and dependent children in your student visa application. Your spouse can work unlimited hours if you are pursuing a master's or PhD, or up to 48 hours per fortnight if you are studying a bachelor's degree.

What is CRICOS and why is it important?

CRICOS (Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students) is the official Australian Government register of education providers and courses for international students. Studying at a CRICOS-registered institution ensures quality education and visa eligibility.
