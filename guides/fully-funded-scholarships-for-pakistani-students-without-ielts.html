How the no-IELTS route works for Pakistani students

Scholarship providers need proof that you can study in English, not a specific test. That is why most accept one of three things:

  1. A Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate from your university confirming your degree was taught in English;
  2. An alternative test such as TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, or the Duolingo English Test;
  3. Program-specific assessment, such as an interview or entrance examination conducted by the scholarship body.

Pakistani students from English-medium institutions have a clear advantage here. Universities like NUST, LUMS, Aga Khan University, IBA Karachi, GIKI, and FAST have well-established records of their MOI certificates being accepted by international scholarship providers, including German, Turkish, Chinese, and Korean programs.

Important: an MOI waiver is not automatic. Always confirm on the official program page whether the provider accepts MOI, and check whether your specific university is on their accepted list before skipping IELTS.

Getting your Medium of Instruction certificate

The MOI certificate is the key that unlocks most no-IELTS applications. Here is how to get one from a Pakistani university:

  1. Contact the right office. Usually the Registrar's office or the Examinations Department issues MOI letters.
  2. Request it in English. Most scholarships need an English letter on official university letterhead.
  3. Order extra copies. You will need one per application, so request several at once.
  4. Translate if needed. If your university issues the certificate in Urdu, arrange an attested or notarized English translation.
  5. Leave time. Universities typically take one to three weeks to process MOI requests.

What a valid MOI certificate usually contains

  • Your full name and registration or roll number
  • The name of your degree and program
  • The dates you studied at the institution
  • An explicit statement that the medium of instruction was English
  • Official letterhead, signature, and a stamp or seal

Alternative English tests accepted by scholarship providers

If your university cannot issue an MOI certificate, or the provider you want insists on a test, these alternatives are widely accepted for fully funded scholarships:

  • TOEFL iBT - accepted by DAAD, US, and most English-taught programs; results in about 6 days.
  • PTE Academic - faster and often cheaper than IELTS, accepted by DAAD and many European universities.
  • Duolingo English Test - fully online, results in 48 hours, increasingly accepted by universities worldwide.
  • HSK (Chinese) - required instead of English for some Chinese-taught CSC programs.
  • TOPIK (Korean) - accepted as an alternative for GKS when applying for Korean-taught programs.

Application steps for Pakistani students

  1. Shortlist programs that match your degree level and accept MOI or an alternative test.
  2. Check the deadline for Pakistani applicants, as some programs set country-specific dates.
  3. Collect your documents: transcripts, degree certificates, MOI letter, passport copy, and reference letters.
  4. Write your motivation letter and statement of purpose using our motivation letter guide and SOP guide.
  5. Apply through the official portal of the scholarship body or the Pakistani embassy, depending on the program.
  6. Prepare for interviews or embassy examinations, which some programs use in place of language tests.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming MOI is accepted everywhere. Chevening and Commonwealth, for example, generally require IELTS or an equivalent even for Pakistani applicants.
  • Submitting a generic MOI letter. It must be on official letterhead, in English, and state the medium of instruction explicitly.
  • Ignoring country-specific deadlines. Programs like GKS and CSC have separate windows for Pakistani applicants.
  • Waiting for IELTS anyway. If a program accepts MOI, do not let the fear of IELTS delay your application.
  • Underestimating the interview. MEXT and some other programs use interviews to assess language, so prepare seriously.

Frequently asked questions

Can Pakistani students get fully funded scholarships without IELTS?

Yes. Many fully funded programs accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate from English-medium Pakistani universities, or alternative tests such as TOEFL, PTE, and Duolingo. These include DAAD, TÃ¼rkiye Burslari, the Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC), the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), MEXT Japan, and Italian Government Scholarships.

What is a Medium of Instruction (MOI) certificate?

An MOI certificate is an official letter from your university confirming that your degree was taught in English. It is accepted by many scholarship providers as proof of English proficiency in place of IELTS. Universities like NUST, LUMS, Aga Khan University, IBA Karachi, GIKI, and FAST have well-established records of MOI acceptance.

How do I get an MOI certificate from my Pakistani university?

Contact your university's registrar or examinations office. Most institutions issue an MOI letter on official letterhead for a small fee. Ask for it in English, request multiple copies, and if the original is in Urdu, arrange a notarized or attested English translation.

Does DAAD require IELTS for Pakistani students?

No, not always. DAAD programs accept TOEFL, PTE, or a Medium of Instruction certificate from your previous university. For the DAAD EPOS program, IELTS, TOEFL, or an MOI certificate from most major Pakistani universities is accepted.

Which scholarships without IELTS are fully funded?

Fully funded options without a hard IELTS requirement include the DAAD scholarships in Germany, TÃ¼rkiye Burslari, the Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC), the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), MEXT scholarships in Japan, and Italian Government Scholarships, all of which cover tuition plus a living stipend.

What alternative English tests are accepted instead of IELTS?

Most providers accept TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and increasingly the Duolingo English Test. Some programs also accept Cambridge certificates, and for language-taught degrees a local language certificate like HSK (Chinese) or TOPIK (Korean) can be used instead.

Is MEXT scholarship without IELTS possible for Pakistani students?

Yes. IELTS is not mandatory for MEXT. English proficiency is assessed through the interview and written examination conducted by the Japanese embassy or consulate during the selection process.

Do Chevening or Commonwealth require IELTS for Pakistanis?

Both Chevening and the Commonwealth Scholarships are English-language programs that generally require an IELTS certificate (or an accepted equivalent) before or shortly after award, so they are not typically considered no-IELTS options for Pakistani students.
