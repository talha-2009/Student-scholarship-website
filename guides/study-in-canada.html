Study in Canada: Complete Guide for International Students

Last updated August 4, 2026 · Reviewed by Abdullah Ijaz Abbasi · 25 min read

Canada has become one of the most popular study destinations in the world, attracting more than 600,000 international students each year. It combines globally respected universities, a famously safe and multicultural society, affordable tuition, generous post-graduation work opportunities, and clear pathways to permanent residency.

This 2026 guide covers the best universities, real costs, scholarships, admission requirements, the study permit, the Post-Graduation Work Permit, working while you study, and permanent residency pathways. Use the table of contents to jump to any section.

Why Study in Canada?

Canada consistently ranks among the top study destinations in the world. Canadian universities are publicly funded and quality-assured, and several — the University of Toronto, UBC, and McGill — sit inside the world's top universities year after year. Canada is also one of the leading countries for research output per capita.

Canada is regularly ranked among the safest countries on earth, and its cities — Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Calgary, and Ottawa — frequently top "most livable city" lists. More than one in five residents was born abroad, so newcomers quickly find communities of their own.

  • Affordable tuition: generally lower than in the US, UK, or Australia, with comparable quality.
  • Post-Graduation Work Permit: work in Canada for up to three years — among the longest post-study work rights in the world.
  • Clear PR pathways: PGWP experience counts toward the Canadian Experience Class and Express Entry; every province runs a Provincial Nominee Program.
  • Co-op programmes: universities like Waterloo built the co-op model, and most institutions now embed paid work terms.

Did you know? Canada is officially bilingual — English and French — and a second official language adds points to your Express Entry profile.

Top Universities in Canada for International Students

Canada has more than 95 universities and 130 public colleges; international applications concentrate on a few globally ranked institutions:

  • University of Toronto (Ontario): Canada's most internationally ranked university, across three campuses. A leader in computer science and AI (home of the Vector Institute), medicine, engineering, and business, and home to the Lester B. Pearson International Scholarship.
  • University of British Columbia (British Columbia): Vancouver and Okanagan campuses, strong in environmental science, sustainability, forestry, and computer science. It offers the Karen McKellin International Leader of Tomorrow Award.
  • McGill University (Quebec): In Montreal, Canada's oldest university and a leader in medicine, law, and the arts. The McCall MacBain Scholarship — Canada's largest graduate award — is headquartered there.
  • University of Waterloo (Ontario): Invented the co-op model, giving many students up to six paid work terms before graduation. Its computer science and mathematics programs feed a vibrant local tech scene.
  • McMaster University (Ontario): In Hamilton, renowned for problem-based learning health sciences and engineering, with health research output among the highest in Canada.
  • University of Alberta (Alberta): In Edmonton, a powerhouse in energy, artificial intelligence (hosting Amii), environmental science, and agriculture.

Choosing a university: compare tuition, scholarships, co-op options, and living costs. A public university with a strong scholarship can beat a higher-ranked one with higher fees.

Cost of Studying in Canada

Tuition ranges from roughly CAD 15,000 to CAD 55,000 per year depending on programme and institution. Humanities sit at the bottom; medicine, dentistry, engineering, and MBAs at the top. Living costs add CAD 10,000 to 15,000.

Admission Requirements

Requirements vary by university and programme, but the pattern below is typical.

Academic requirements

  • Undergraduate: secondary education equivalent to a Canadian high school diploma, usually with a 60–85% average; competitive programmes want the highest grades and specific prerequisites.
  • Master's: a relevant bachelor's with a competitive GPA (about 3.0 on a 4.0 scale); some professional programmes want work experience.
  • PhD: a relevant master's with a research component, a strong proposal, and usually a supervisor match.

Transcripts and credential evaluation

Degrees earned outside Canada require an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for universities and IRCC; WES (World Education Services) and ICAS (International Credential Assessment Service of Canada) are the services most commonly used.

English language requirements

  • IELTS Academic: 6.5–7.0 overall (no band below 6.0), higher for competitive graduate programmes.
  • TOEFL iBT: 80–93 overall, depending on the programme.
  • Duolingo English Test: accepted by a growing number of universities, usually 110–130.
  • Other tests: PTE and CAEL are widely accepted, and some universities accept a medium-of-instruction certificate. See our scholarships without IELTS guide.

Standardised tests, SOP, and references

  • GRE / GMAT: required for some graduate programmes — the GRE for many science and engineering master's, the GMAT for MBA.
  • Statement of Purpose: most graduate applications need a 500–1,000 word statement. Use our SOP writing guide.
  • Reference letters: two to three academic letters are standard. See our recommendation letter guide.

Study Permit: How to Apply

International students must hold a study permit issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) unless exempt. It lets you study and work part-time. Here is the process:

  1. Get your letter of acceptance (LOA) from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI).
  2. Check the requirements: prove you are a genuine student with enough funds and are admissible to Canada.
  3. Show proof of funds: at least CAD 20,635 for living costs (outside Quebec), plus first-year tuition and return travel.
  4. Give biometrics (fingerprints and photo) at a Visa Application Centre and pay the CAD 85 fee.
  5. Apply online through the IRCC portal with your LOA, proof of funds, and biometrics receipt.
  6. Wait for processing — several weeks to a few months — so apply 6 to 12 months ahead.

Student Direct Stream (SDS) and the GIC

The Student Direct Stream (SDS) was a faster route for students from eligible countries, historically requiring a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) of CAD 10,000, a higher English score, and upfront tuition. IRCC has since ended SDS. A GIC of at least CAD 20,635 still serves as proof of funds.

Visa tips: do not book flights until your permit is approved. The busiest processing period is April to August. See our complete student visa guide.

Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

The Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) is an open work permit for graduates of qualifying programmes at designated institutions — work for any employer in Canada without a job offer.

How long the PGWP lasts

  • Programmes of 8 months to under 2 years: the PGWP matches the programme length.
  • Programmes of 2 years or more: the full three years.
  • Programmes under 8 months are not eligible.

Eligibility

  • You studied full-time at a DLI and completed the programme.
  • You apply within 180 days of completing your programme.
  • Your study permit was valid throughout.

From PGWP to permanent residency

PGWP experience feeds into the Canadian Experience Class (one year, or 1,560 hours, of skilled work) and Provincial Nominee Programs, which add 600 points to your Express Entry profile — effectively guaranteeing an invitation. Express Entry's Comprehensive Ranking System rewards age, education, language, and Canadian work experience.

Plan the three years: the PGWP is issued only once. Use year one for skilled work, year two to improve your language score and research provincial streams, and apply for PR before it expires.

Working While Studying

Working part-time is one of the biggest financial advantages of choosing Canada. The rules are written on your study permit:

  • Off-campus: up to 20 hours per week during regular sessions, for any employer, without a separate permit.
  • Scheduled breaks: full-time work — 40 hours per week — during winter holidays, reading weeks, and summer.
  • On-campus: unlimited hours on campus, including university shops and services.
  • Co-op: mandatory paid work terms need a separate co-op work permit, arranged with your university.

A student working 20 hours a week at minimum wage earns roughly CAD 900 to CAD 1,100 a month.

Popular part-time jobs for students

  • On-campus: library, research, and teaching assistant
  • Retail and hospitality: cashier, barista, server
  • Delivery and rideshare apps
  • Administrative: reception and data entry
  • Tutoring in your strongest subjects

Intakes and Application Timeline

Most Canadian universities offer two or three intakes per year:

  • Fall (September): the main intake, with the most courses and scholarships. Apply 8 to 12 months ahead.
  • Winter (January): a smaller secondary intake. Apply 6 to 8 months ahead.
  • Summer (May): fewer programmes, but common for colleges and some graduate programs.

Recommended timeline

  • 12–18 months before: shortlist universities and courses; research scholarships.
  • 9–12 months before: take English tests; collect transcripts; apply.
  • 6–9 months before: receive offers and pay deposits; begin the study permit application.
  • 3–6 months before: submit biometrics; book flights; arrange insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much does it cost to study in Canada per year?

Most students budget CAD 35,000 to 65,000 per year. Tuition is CAD 15,000 to 55,000 and living costs add CAD 10,000 to 15,000.

What is the minimum IELTS score required to study in Canada?

Most universities ask for IELTS Academic 6.5 to 7.0 overall (no band below 6.0) or TOEFL iBT 80 to 93. Competitive programmes often require 7.0 or higher.

Can I study in Canada without IELTS?

Yes. Many universities accept the Duolingo English Test, PTE, and CAEL, and some accept a medium-of-instruction certificate if your degree was taught in English. Confirm with the university first.

Do I need a study permit to study in Canada?

Yes. International students need a study permit from IRCC unless exempt — a letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution, proof of funds, and usually biometrics.

How much money do I need to show for a Canadian study permit?

As of 2026 you must show at least CAD 20,635 for living costs (outside Quebec), plus your first year of tuition and return travel, and more for any dependants.

Can international students work while studying in Canada?

Yes. You can work up to 20 hours per week off-campus during regular sessions and full-time (40 hours) during scheduled breaks. Co-op work needs a separate permit.

How long is the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) valid?

The PGWP is issued for the length of your programme, up to a maximum of three years. Programmes of at least eight months qualify; two-year programmes get the full three years.

How can I get permanent residency after studying in Canada?

The main routes are the Canadian Experience Class (after one year of skilled work), Provincial Nominee Programs (which add 600 points to Express Entry), and the Federal Skilled Worker Program.

What are the intake periods for Canadian universities?

Most universities offer Fall (September), Winter (January), and sometimes Summer (May) intakes. Fall is the main intake; apply 8 to 12 months ahead.

Study in Canada Checklist

  • Have I shortlisted universities, programmes, and an intake?
  • Is my university a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)?
  • Have I taken an accepted English test (IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo)?
  • Have I applied for scholarships, including entrance awards?
  • Have I arranged an ECA (WES or ICAS) if needed?
  • Are my transcripts, statement of purpose, and references ready?
  • Do I have my letter of acceptance?
  • Have I applied for my study permit with proof of funds (CAD 20,635)?
  • Have I planned my budget, accommodation, and health insurance?
