Study in Japan 2026: Complete Guide for International Students

Published July 24, 2026 · Updated for 2026

Japan offers international students a world-class education rooted in innovation, precision, and cultural depth. With seven Nobel Prize-winning universities, cutting-edge research facilities, and the Japanese government's generous MEXT Scholarship, Japan has become one of Asia's most attractive study destinations. This guide covers everything you need to know about studying in Japan in 2026, including top universities, scholarship options, tuition costs, visa procedures, and practical advice for daily life.

Why Study in Japan?

Japan is a global leader in technology, robotics, automotive engineering, environmental science, and biomedical research. Japanese universities combine rigorous academic standards with hands-on research opportunities, producing graduates who are highly sought after by employers worldwide.

Beyond academics, Japan offers one of the safest environments for international students. The country consistently ranks among the safest in the world, with low crime rates, reliable public transportation, and a healthcare system that covers international students through the National Health Insurance programme. The cost of living is also reasonable compared to other developed study destinations like the United States, United Kingdom, or Australia.

Japan is also the only Asian country with multiple universities consistently ranked among the global top 100. The Japanese government actively promotes internationalisation through the "Top Global University Project" and the MEXT scholarship programme, which has funded over 100,000 international students since its inception.

MEXT Scholarship

The MEXT Scholarship (Monbukagakusho) is the Japanese government's flagship scholarship programme for international students. It is one of the most generous scholarships in the world, covering full tuition, a monthly living stipend, and round-trip airfare.

MEXT scholarships are available at four levels: undergraduate (5 years including preparatory year), master’s (2 years), PhD (3 years), and research (short-term to 2 years). The monthly stipend ranges from approximately ¥143,000 for undergraduate students to ¥147,000 for graduate students, which is sufficient to cover living expenses in most Japanese cities.

There are two application tracks for the MEXT scholarship:

  • Embassy recommendation: Apply through the Japanese embassy or consulate in your home country. This track involves a written examination and interview. The embassy nomination deadline is typically between March and May each year.
  • University recommendation: Apply directly to a Japanese university, which then nominates you to MEXT. This track is more competitive but allows you to contact professors and prepare a research proposal before applying.

For full details on eligibility, required documents, and the application timeline, visit our dedicated MEXT Scholarship page.

Cost of Studying in Japan

Tuition fees in Japan vary significantly between national, public, and private universities. National universities are regulated by the government and charge the same tuition across all institutions, while private universities set their own fees.

Admission Requirements

Admission requirements vary by university and programme level, but the following are generally required.

  • Academic qualifications: 12 years of education for undergraduate programmes; a bachelor's degree for master's programmes; a master's degree for PhD programmes.
  • Language proficiency: Japanese-taught programmes require JLPT N2 or higher. English-taught programmes require IELTS 6.0+ or TOEFL iBT 80+.
  • Entrance examinations: Many universities require their own entrance exams, especially for undergraduate programmes. Some accept EJU (Examination for Japanese University Admission) scores.
  • Research proposal: Required for all graduate programme applications. Your proposal should align with the research interests of a faculty member at the target university.
  • Letters of recommendation: Most universities require two to three letters from academic referees.

Student Visa Process

International students accepted into a Japanese university must obtain a student visa before entering Japan. The process typically takes one to three months.

  1. Receive your Certificate of Eligibility (CoE): Your university applies for the CoE on your behalf from the Immigration Bureau of Japan. This confirms you meet the conditions for studying in Japan.
  2. Apply for the visa: Submit your CoE, passport, visa application form, photographs, and any additional documents to the Japanese embassy or consulate in your country.
  3. Receive your visa: The student visa is typically valid for one year and can be renewed. If you are on a MEXT scholarship, the visa process is usually expedited.
  4. Register as a resident: After arriving in Japan, you must register your address at the local municipal office within 14 days.

See our Student Visa Guide for more detailed information on visa applications.

Student Life in Japan

Life as an international student in Japan is both exciting and practical. Japanese universities offer extensive support services for international students, including orientation programmes, language classes, housing assistance, and cultural activities.

Japan's public transportation system is excellent, making it easy to explore the country during breaks. Students can purchase discounted commuter passes and, in many cities, bicycle is a convenient and affordable mode of transport.

The National Health Insurance (NHI) programme requires all international students staying longer than three months to enrol. The insurance covers 70% of medical costs, and the annual premium is approximately ¥20,000 to ¥40,000 depending on your city and income level.

Accommodation options include university dormitories (the most affordable), shared apartments, and private apartments. Many universities guarantee dormitory housing for first-year international students.

Working While Studying in Japan

International students on a student visa are permitted to work part-time. You must obtain a work permit from the immigration office, which your university can help you apply for. Once approved, you can work up to 28 hours per week during academic terms and up to 40 hours per week during long holidays.

Common part-time jobs for international students include teaching assistant, research assistant, tutoring, retail, food service, and translation work. Students proficient in Japanese will have access to a wider range of job opportunities with higher pay. The minimum wage varies by prefecture but ranges from ¥850 to ¥1,100 per hour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I study in Japan without knowing Japanese?

Yes. Many Japanese universities offer English-taught programmes, especially at the graduate level. These are known as SGUs (Super Global Universities). You will still benefit from learning basic Japanese for daily life, and most universities offer free Japanese language classes for international students.

How do I apply for the MEXT scholarship?

Apply through embassy recommendation (visit the Japanese embassy in your country) or university recommendation (contact a Japanese university directly). Both tracks require strong academic records and a well-prepared research or study plan. See our MEXT Scholarship page for full details.

How much does studying in Japan cost?

National universities charge about ¥535,800 per year. Private universities charge ¥800,000 to ¥2,000,000. Living costs range from ¥100,000 to ¥180,000 per month depending on the city. The MEXT scholarship covers all tuition and provides a monthly stipend.

What is the JLPT and do I need it?

The JLPT (Japanese Language Proficiency Test) has five levels from N5 (beginner) to N1 (advanced). Japanese-taught programmes typically require N2 or higher. For English-taught programmes, IELTS or TOEFL is required instead. Most students can reach N2 with 12-18 months of dedicated study.

Can I work while studying in Japan?

Yes. International students on a student visa can work up to 28 hours per week during term and up to 40 hours per week during holidays. You need a work permit from immigration, which your university can help you obtain.
