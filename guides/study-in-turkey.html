Study in Turkey: Complete Guide for International Students

Last updated August 4, 2026 · Reviewed by Abdullah Ijaz Abbasi · 12 min read

Turkey has become one of the world's fastest-growing study destinations, now hosting well over 300,000 international students and adding more every year. Students come from South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and the Balkans to study medicine, engineering, business, and social sciences at a fraction of the cost of Western universities — and many of them pay nothing at all thanks to the country's flagship scholarship programme, Türkiye Bursları.

Turkey's appeal is easy to understand. Public universities charge international students remarkably low tuition, private foundation universities offer English-taught programmes at Western academic standards, living costs remain among the most affordable in Europe, and the Turkish government actively funds international students with one of the most comprehensive scholarship packages anywhere. Add in a young, dynamic society, modern campuses, and a country bridging Europe and Asia, and it is no surprise that Turkey is now a serious alternative to the usual Western destinations.

This complete 2026 guide covers why Turkey is worth considering, its most respected universities, the Türkiye Bursları government scholarship in full detail, real tuition and living costs with comparison tables, English-taught programmes, admission requirements, the student visa and residence permit process, working rules, and the application timeline. Why Study in Turkey?

Turkey invests heavily in higher education and in attracting international talent. The number of foreign students in Turkey has grown steadily for a decade, supported by state policy that treats education as a form of soft power — and that ambition translates into real benefits for students.

  • The Türkiye Bursları scholarship: The Turkish government fully funds thousands of international students every year, covering tuition, a monthly stipend, a free Turkish language year, housing, health insurance, and flights. Very few countries offer such a complete package.
  • Low tuition at public universities: International tuition at many public universities is roughly USD 300–1,000 per year, a fraction of what comparable programmes cost in the US, UK, or Australia.
  • English-taught programmes: English-medium degrees are widespread, particularly at private and foundation universities, so you can begin studying without knowing Turkish.
  • Respected universities: Institutions like Boğaziçi, Koç, Sabancı, Bilkent, METU, and Istanbul Technical University are well known internationally, especially in engineering, computer science, and business.
  • Affordable living: With living costs of roughly USD 400–700 per month, Turkey is one of the most budget-friendly study destinations in the broader European region.
  • Cultural and strategic position: Istanbul straddles two continents, and Turkey offers a safe, multicultural environment with a strong Turkish diaspora community in many applicant countries.

Did you know? Turkey's university system is one of the largest in the world by enrolment, and the country has made attracting international students an explicit national goal. That commitment shows in the scale of Türkiye Bursları, which accepts new fully funded scholars from around 150 countries every year.

Türkiye Bursları (Turkey Government Scholarship)

Türkiye Bursları (also written Turkiye Burslari, and known in English as the Turkey Government Scholarship) is the Turkish government's flagship fully funded scholarship for international students. It is run by the Presidency for Turks Abroad and Related Communities (YTB) and administered through the official portal turkiyeburslari.gov.tr. In one application you can be placed at a university of your choice or one selected for you, covering every major expense.

What the scholarship covers

  • Full tuition: The government pays your entire tuition at a Turkish university for the full duration of your degree.
  • Monthly stipend: Scholars receive a monthly stipend to cover day-to-day living, set each year by the programme.
  • Free one-year Turkish language course: Scholars who are not fluent in Turkish receive a year of free language preparation at a TÖMER centre before starting their degree.
  • Accommodation: University dormitory accommodation is provided, with the cost covered.
  • Health insurance: Scholars are covered by health insurance for the duration of their studies.
  • Round-trip flight: The programme covers the flight from your home country at the start and end of your studies.

When and how to apply

Applications open once a year, typically in January, and close in February or March. You apply entirely online at turkiyeburslari.gov.tr — there is no application fee and no intermediary is needed. You select your preferred programmes and universities, upload your documents, and, if shortlisted, attend an interview. Read the full details and current requirements on our dedicated Türkiye Bursları page.

Best strategy: Türkiye Bursları is extremely competitive, so apply the day applications open and make your motivation letter count — see our motivation letter guide. In parallel, apply directly to universities through their international offices, since many run independent admission cycles with their own merit scholarships.

Tuition is where Turkey shines. Public universities charge international students far less than almost any equivalent Western institution, while private foundation universities price themselves closer to international norms but still below US and UK levels. English-Taught Programmes

You do not need to speak Turkish to study in Turkey. English-medium programmes are common and expanding, with the strongest concentration at private and foundation universities.

  • Foundation universities: Koç, Sabancı, Bilkent, and many other foundation universities teach most or all of their programmes in English, from engineering to business to medicine.
  • Public universities: Boğaziçi University offers numerous English-medium programmes, and METU (ODTÜ) and Istanbul Technical University (İTÜ) run large English-medium faculties, especially in engineering and the sciences.
  • Graduate level: Most international master's and PhD programmes in engineering, computer science, and business are available in English at both public and foundation universities.

For admission to an English-taught programme you will normally need an English proficiency certificate — typically IELTS or TOEFL. Some universities also accept other tests or run their own placement assessment, so always check the specific programme page.

Language strategy: If you choose a Turkish-taught programme, most universities offer a free or low-cost preparatory Turkish year, and Türkiye Bursları covers it fully. Even if your degree is in English, learning conversational Turkish makes daily life, internships, and friendships dramatically easier.

Admission Requirements

Requirements differ by university and level, but the pattern below is typical for international applicants in Turkey.

Undergraduate

  • Academic record: Secondary school transcripts and certificate equivalent to the Turkish high school diploma; universities compare your country's grading system.
  • Entry exams: Some universities require the YÖS (Foreign Student Examination) for international undergraduate admission, while others accept the SAT instead. Check each university's requirement — it is not universal.
  • Language: Turkish proficiency (TÖMER) or a preparatory year for Turkish-taught programmes; IELTS/TOEFL for English-taught ones.

Graduate (Master's and PhD)

  • Transcripts and degree: A recognised bachelor's (or master's for PhD) degree with good grades.
  • Reference letters: Usually two letters — see our recommendation letter guide.
  • Statement of purpose: A clear statement of why you want the programme — see our motivation letter guide.
  • CV and, for some programmes, a research proposal or portfolio.

Student Visa and Residence Permit

International students need two steps: a student visa to enter Turkey, then a residence permit after arrival. See our full student visa guide for general guidance.

  1. Get your admission letter: You must first be accepted by a Turkish university, which issues the documents needed for the visa.
  2. Apply for the Student Visa (type D): Submit your application at the Turkish embassy or consulate in your home country. You will need your admission letter, passport, and evidence of finances and health insurance.
  3. Travel to Turkey: Enter with your student visa, then act quickly.
  4. Apply for the residence permit (ikamet): Register with the local immigration authority within 30 days of arrival to legalise your stay for the academic year.
  5. Arrange health insurance: Health insurance is mandatory for international students in Turkey, either through the university or a private provider.

Important: The 30-day residence permit window is strictly enforced. Start the ikamet application the day you arrive, and keep copies of everything. Missing the deadline can disrupt your studies and your visa status.

Working While Studying

International students can work part-time in Turkey, but the practical reality is that opportunities are limited compared with the US or Australia.

  • Work permit required: International students must obtain a work permit before starting a job, which many employers are unwilling to arrange for part-time, short-term roles.
  • Practical limits: In practice, most international students cover their living costs through scholarships, family support, or savings rather than wages.
  • On-campus options: Some universities offer paid on-campus jobs, research assistantships, and tutoring roles that are easier to secure than external jobs.
  • After graduation: Graduates of Turkish universities are generally attractive to Turkish employers, and a residence permit extension can be sought for a job search.

Türkiye Bursları scholars are the most protected group — their stipend is designed to cover living costs so they do not need to work. For everyone else, budget conservatively and treat work as a bonus, not a necessity.

Intakes and Application Timeline

Turkish universities run two main intakes, with Fall being the dominant one for international students.

  • Fall (September): The main intake for most undergraduate and graduate programmes, and the intake aligned with Türkiye Bursları placement.
  • Spring (February / winter): Some universities and departments admit students for a second, smaller intake.

Recommended timeline

  • 12 months before: Shortlist universities and programmes; start IELTS/TOEFL or Turkish preparation; check YÖS/SAT requirements.
  • 9–10 months before: Apply to Türkiye Bursları when it opens in January; sit any required university exams; prepare transcripts and references.
  • 6–9 months before: Apply directly to universities; await offers; accept and pay any deposit.
  • 3–6 months before: Apply for the student visa, then the residence permit after arrival; arrange accommodation and health insurance.

Because Türkiye Bursları closes in February or March, mark it early in your calendar. For more fully funded options worldwide, see our list of top fully funded scholarships and the fully funded scholarships guide.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much does it cost to study in Turkey per year?

Tuition at public universities typically costs USD 300 to 1,000 per year, while private and foundation universities charge USD 5,000 to 25,000 or more. Living costs add roughly USD 400 to 700 per month, so most students at public universities spend between USD 5,000 and 10,000 per year in total.

What is Turkiye Burslari and who can apply?

Turkiye Burslari (Turkey Government Scholarships) is the flagship fully funded scholarship funded by the Turkish government. International students of all nationalities who are not Turkish citizens and are applying to a Turkish university for the first time can apply. The application typically opens around January and closes in February or March each year.

Is Turkiye Burslari fully funded?

Yes. Turkiye Burslari covers full tuition, a monthly stipend, a free one-year Turkish language course, accommodation, health insurance, and a round-trip flight ticket. It is one of the most comprehensive fully funded scholarships in the world.

Can I study in Turkey in English?

Yes. English-taught programmes are common, especially at private and foundation universities such as Koc and Sabanci, and several public institutions including Bogazici, METU, and Istanbul Technical University run English-medium programmes. You will typically need IELTS or TOEFL for admission.

Do I need to know Turkish to study in Turkey?

For Turkish-taught programmes you need Tomer or Turkish proficiency, or you can complete the free preparatory Turkish language year that many universities (including Turkiye Burslari scholars) receive. For English-taught programmes, English proficiency such as IELTS or TOEFL is required instead.

How do I get a student visa for Turkey?

Apply for a Turkish Student Visa (type D) at the Turkish embassy or consulate in your home country using your admission letter and supporting documents. After arriving, you must register for a residence permit (ikamet) within 30 days, and health insurance is required.

Can international students work while studying in Turkey?

Yes, but in practice the opportunities are limited. International students can work part-time only after obtaining a work permit, and most funding for living costs comes from scholarships or personal funds rather than part-time wages.

What is the YOS exam for studying in Turkey?

YOS (Foreign Student Examination) is used by some Turkish universities for international undergraduate admission. Not all universities use it; some accept the SAT, and graduate programmes rely on transcripts, reference letters, and a statement instead.

What is the monthly living cost for a student in Turkey?

Living costs are roughly USD 400 to 700 per month depending on the city. Istanbul is the most expensive, Ankara and Izmir sit in the middle, and cities in central and eastern Anatolia are noticeably cheaper, especially for accommodation.

What are the intake periods for Turkish universities?

The Fall (September) intake is the main intake for most programmes. Some universities and departments also offer a Spring (winter) admission. Turkiye Burslari applications open around January and close in February or March for the following academic year.
