Bachelor's funding - Switzerland

Undergraduate Scholarships for International Students in Switzerland

Switzerland is a confusing market for bachelor's funding: the government scholarship everyone knows covers only graduate study, while the public universities that welcome undergraduates charge a fraction of what their English-speaking peers do. The reality is that undergraduate scholarships for international students in Switzerland exist, but you need to know exactly where to look. This guide maps every option, the honest costs, and the visa requirements that shape your budget.

The honest picture of Swiss bachelor's funding

If you search for "Switzerland scholarships" you will mostly find graduate programs. That is not an accident: the Swiss system concentrates public funding at master's, doctoral, and postdoctoral level. The flagship Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships fund research, PhD, and postdoctoral candidates, plus a small art master's track, and are not available for bachelor's students.

For international undergraduates, the financial logic is different. Public universities charge low tuition precisely so that degrees remain affordable without large scholarship programs. ETH Zurich and EPFL raised fees for non-Swiss and non-EU students to CHF 2,190 per semester from autumn 2025, but that is still well below the costs at comparable universities in the United States, the United Kingdom, or Australia.

What this means for you

  • Bachelor's scholarships for international students in Switzerland are scarce but real.
  • The main options are university-level awards, not government programs.
  • Low public tuition is the real "funding" most international students rely on.
  • Budget for living costs: they, not tuition, are the largest expense.

Undergraduate scholarships that accept international students

These are the realistic bachelor-level funding routes for international students.

USI bachelor scholarships

The UniversitÃ  della Svizzera italiana (USI) in Lugano is one of the few Swiss universities with a genuine bachelor scholarship program. It includes first-year bachelor scholarships, bachelor scholarships for students domiciled in Ticino or abroad, and the Leonardo Foundation Scholarship for International Students on the Mendrisio campus. Deadlines typically fall in the spring, so apply in the academic year before you start. See the official USI grants page for the current list and application forms.

University of Basel scholarship funds

The University of Basel administers need-based support through its Scholarship Funds and the Legat Grobe, which are available to bachelor's and master's students, including international students in financial difficulty. Awards are needs-based rather than merit-based, and you usually apply through the university after enrollment.

Franklin University Switzerland merit awards

Franklin University Switzerland, a private liberal arts university, awards merit scholarships for undergraduate applicants. Its Franklin Honours Programme Award offers between US$3,000 and US$10,000 per year to strong applicants, with an early December priority deadline. As a private institution, Franklin charges significantly higher tuition than public universities, so weigh the net cost carefully.

Private hospitality and business schools

Swiss hospitality schools such as EHL and the Swiss Education Group institutions offer merit and needs-based awards for bachelor programs in hospitality management. These are private institutions with high tuition, and scholarships are typically partial.

Best strategy for most students: target low-tuition public programs as your base, apply for the university awards listed above, and plan to fund living costs through family support, savings, loans, and the part-time work allowed on a student permit. Trying to find a single "fully funded" Swiss bachelor's scholarship will waste time.

Where international students study for a bachelor's in Switzerland

Swiss public universities admit international undergraduates, though competition for limited seats is strong and admission is selective. The main public options include:

  • ETH Zurich and EPFL - two federal institutes of technology, famous for engineering, natural sciences, and architecture; bachelor programs are taught in German (ETH) or French (EPFL), with some English-taught options.
  • University of Zurich, Geneva, Basel, Bern, Lausanne - large cantonal universities with a wide range of bachelor programs, mostly taught in German or French.
  • University of St. Gallen (HSG) - strong in business, economics, and law, with English-taught bachelor tracks.
  • USI Lugano - Italian-speaking Switzerland's university, with English-taught bachelor programs in informatics, economics, communication, and architecture.

Because most public bachelor programs are taught in German, French, or Italian, language proficiency is often a bigger barrier than funding. English-taught bachelor options are concentrated at USI, HSG, and private institutions.

Visa proof of funds and part-time work

For a student visa you generally need to show access to around CHF 21,000 per year for living costs, on top of tuition. Some cantons require the amount to be held in a blocked account. Requirements vary by canton and nationality, so confirm the current figure with the Swiss embassy or consulate where you apply.

International students from non-EU/EFTA countries may work up to 15 hours per week during term and full-time during official holidays, subject to a work permit. EU/EFTA students face fewer restrictions. Part-time work is genuinely useful for covering a share of living costs, but do not plan to finance a degree on it: between a full course load and limited hours, it will not close the gap on its own.

How to apply and improve your chances

  1. Choose your language track first. Confirm which bachelor programs you can realistically study in based on your German, French, Italian, or English level.
  2. Check admission requirements early. Swiss universities evaluate your secondary school certificate against the Swiss MaturitÃ©; some require additional exams or a foundation year for certain school systems.
  3. Apply to university scholarships in parallel. USI and Franklin awards have separate deadlines from admission, so submit both on time.
  4. Prepare proof of funds. Arrange the blocked account or financial documentation needed for the visa application several months in advance.
  5. Plan living costs honestly. Build a monthly budget covering rent, food, health insurance, and transport, then identify the gap your scholarship, savings, and part-time work must fill.

Frequently asked questions

Are there undergraduate scholarships in Switzerland for international students?

Yes, but they are limited compared with graduate funding. The main bachelor-level options are USI bachelor scholarships, the University of Basel Scholarship Funds and Legat Grobe, and merit awards at private institutions like Franklin University Switzerland. Most Swiss government funding, including the Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships, is reserved for master's, PhD, and postdoctoral applicants.

How much does it cost to study in Switzerland as an international undergraduate?

Public university tuition is remarkably low, typically CHF 1,000 to about CHF 4,380 per year. ETH Zurich and EPFL raised fees for non-Swiss and non-EU students to CHF 2,190 per semester from autumn 2025. The real cost is living expenses, which run around CHF 1,600 to CHF 2,500 per month depending on the city.

Does the Swiss Government Excellence Scholarship cover bachelor's degrees?

No. The Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships (administered by FCS/ESKAS) fund research and PhD scholarships, postdoctoral stays, and selected art master's programs. They do not fund bachelor's degrees for international students.

Check the official USI grants page for eligibility and deadlines.

What universities offer undergraduate scholarships in Switzerland?

The main options for international undergraduates are USI (UniversitÃ  della Svizzera italiana), the University of Basel, and private institutions such as Franklin University Switzerland and the Swiss Education Group hospitality schools. ETH Zurich and EPFL focus their scholarship funding on master's students.

Can international students work part-time while studying in Switzerland?

Students from non-EU/EFTA countries can work up to 15 hours per week during term and full-time during official holidays, subject to a work permit. EU/EFTA students face fewer restrictions. Part-time income can cover a meaningful share of living costs but is rarely enough to fund a degree on its own.

How much money do I need to prove for a Swiss student visa?

For a Swiss student visa you generally need to prove access to around CHF 21,000 per year for living costs, in addition to paying tuition. Some cantons require the amount to be held in a blocked account. Requirements can change, so confirm with the Swiss embassy in your home country.
