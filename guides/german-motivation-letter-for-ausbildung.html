German Motivation Letter for Ausbildung: Complete Guide with Free Sample & Template

Last updated August 1, 2026 · 22 min read

A German Motivation Letter for Ausbildung is a one-page formal letter in which you explain why you want a specific apprenticeship in Germany, why you chose this occupation, and why you chose a particular company. German employers expect it together with your CV and school certificates, and in 2026 it is still the single most important document in a successful vocational training application.

If you are an international student or young professional dreaming of doing dual vocational training (Ausbildung) in Germany, this guide is for you. It covers everything: the exact German format, a step-by-step writing process, a complete copy-and-paste sample, six ready-to-adapt examples for popular professions, the German phrases that impress recruiters, and the mistakes that get applications thrown away. You will also find free Word, PDF, and text templates you can download at the bottom of this page.

Key takeaways

  • Length: one DIN A4 page, roughly 300-500 words.
  • Language: write in German; B1 is the usual minimum, B2 for healthcare.
  • Structure: salutation, opening, background, why this profession, why this company, future plans, polite closing.
  • Documents: always send the letter with your CV and school certificates as one PDF.
  • Timing: apply 9-12 months early; applications often open between August and October for the following autumn.
  • Tone: formal German ("Sie"), handwritten signature, zero spelling errors.

What is an Ausbildung Motivation Letter?

An Ausbildung motivation letter (Motivationsschreiben or Anschreiben) is a formal one-page letter that convinces a German employer to invite you for an interview for a vocational training position. It is the German-speaking equivalent of a cover letter, but it is written specifically for the dual vocational training system, in which you work at a company roughly three to four days a week and attend vocational school (Berufsschule) on the remaining days.

Ausbildung is Germany's world-famous dual vocational training model. Around 320 recognised training occupations exist, from electrician and industrial mechanic to IT specialist, nurse, hotel specialist, and office administrator. Trainees receive a monthly salary, learn on the job, and graduate with a qualification that is recognised across Germany and often in the wider EU.

Because the company invests time and money in you, the motivation letter has one job: prove that you are reliable, genuinely interested in the trade, able to handle the work, and likely to finish the whole apprenticeship. If you are applying from abroad, the letter also needs to reassure the employer about your German level and your intention to stay.

Why German Employers Ask for One

German employers ask for a motivation letter because they are making a long-term hiring decision and the letter is their main way to judge your character and commitment. A vocational training contract usually lasts two to three-and-a-half years, and the company spends money on your salary, training, and supervision throughout that period. A weak application can cost them tens of thousands of euros.

In addition, the letter reveals things a CV cannot:

  • Motivation: Did you choose this profession deliberately, or are you applying everywhere?
  • Reliability: A well-formatted, error-free letter suggests a reliable trainee who will show up on time.
  • Understanding of the job: Recruiters look for realistic expectations about the daily work, not romanticised ideas.
  • Fit with the company: Employers want trainees who fit their team and will stay after finishing.
  • Language and integration (for international applicants): The letter is a de facto German test. Good written German dramatically improves your chances.

This is why we always recommend writing the letter yourself, in your own words. German human-resources teams are highly trained at spotting template letters, and one in three applications is rejected within the first minutes because it is obviously mass-produced.

German Motivation Letter Format

The standard German motivation letter format is one DIN A4 page, printed, with your contact block at the top, a subject line, a formal salutation, three to five short paragraphs, and a handwritten signature.

German formatting is more rigid than in many other countries, so follow it precisely. The table below shows the exact structure from top to bottom:

PositionWhat goes thereExample / note
1. Your contact blockFull name, address, phone, email, and dateTop right corner; German date format: 01.08.2026
2. Subject lineThe position and start date"Bewerbung um einen Ausbildungsplatz als Fachinformatiker ab August 2027"
3. SalutationFormal "Sie" address"Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren" or "Sehr geehrter Herr Müller"
4. OpeningThe apprenticeship + how you found itOne short sentence that shows you read the advert
5. BackgroundSchool, relevant subjects, internshipsConcrete subjects and grades, not general statements
6. Why this professionYour interest in the trade itselfTie it to real experience, a hobby, or a role model
7. Why this companySpecific reasons for choosing this employerMention the website, a product, the team, or their training reputation
8. ClosingConfidence, availability, interview request"Über eine Einladung zu einem persönlichen Gespräch freue ich mich sehr."
9. Sign-offFormal closing + signature"Mit freundlichen Grüßen" then your handwritten signature

Font and spacing: use Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman at 11-12pt, single or 1.15 line spacing, with normal margins. Print on white paper and sign by hand in blue or black ink. If you email the application, scan the signed page into your single PDF.

Step-by-Step Writing Guide

Follow these seven steps, and you will produce a letter that looks like it was written by a German career coach.

  1. Research the company and the job advertisement. Before writing a single word, read the full advertisement twice. Note the start date, the exact job title, and any requirements such as school-leaving grade or language level. Visit the company website and write down one or two specific facts about them.
  2. Collect your raw material. Write bullet lists of: your school subjects and grades, internships or part-time jobs, technical or manual skills, languages, and one or two personal qualities with a short example for each.
  3. Write the opening line. State the exact apprenticeship and start date in one sentence. Example: "mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Anzeige für den Ausbildungsplatz als Elektroniker für Gebäudetechnik ab September 2027 gelesen."
  4. Add your background paragraph. Mention your current or most recent school, your strongest subjects (especially math, German, and science or IT-related subjects), and any internship. Use facts: "In Mathematik stehe ich durchschnittlich bei 2,0" sounds much better than "ich bin gut in Mathe".
  5. Write the "why this profession" and "why this company" paragraphs. This is the heart of the letter. Explain where your interest comes from (a school project, a family workshop, a holiday job) and why you specifically chose this company (their products, training programme, or location).
  6. Close with your future plans and a request for an interview. Say you are confident you can complete the training, mention that you can start on the required date, and ask for an interview.
  7. Revise, proofread, and get a second opinion. Read it aloud, check every umlaut, and ask a German-speaking friend or a tutor to review it. Spelling errors are the fastest way to be rejected.

Below is a complete, ready-to-use German motivation letter for Ausbildung. Replace everything in square brackets, personalise the company paragraph, and do not submit it before you have rewritten it in your own words.

Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,

mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Anzeige für den Ausbildungsplatz als [Berufsbezeichnung] ab [Monat Jahr] gelesen. Da ich mich seit mehreren Jahren für [Bereich] begeistere, bewerbe ich mich hiermit sehr gern bei Ihrem Unternehmen.

Zurzeit besuche ich die [Schulname] in [Ort] und werde die mittlere Reife im Sommer [Jahr] abschließen. In den Fächern [Fach 1] und [Fach 2] stehe ich im oberen Leistungsbereich. Ein zweiwöchiges Praktikum bei [Firma] im Bereich [Bereich] hat mir gezeigt, dass mir die praktische Arbeit große Freude bereitet und ich zuverlässig und sorgfältig arbeite.

Ihr Unternehmen hat mich besonders überzeugt, weil [konkreter Grund, z. B. moderne Ausbildungsabteilung / bekanntes Produkt / gutes Ausbildungskonzept]. Genau hier möchte ich meine beruflichen Fähigkeiten aufbauen und langfristig Teil Ihres Teams werden.

Über eine Einladung zu einem persönlichen Gespräch freue ich mich sehr. Ich bin ab [Datum] verfügbar und kann die Ausbildung zu dem gewünschten Termin beginnen.

Mit freundlichen Grüßen

[Handschriftliche Unterschrift]
[Vorname Nachname]

Line by line, here is what this sample does: the subject line names the exact apprenticeship and start date, the opening shows the advert was actually read, the background paragraph proves school performance with a concrete internship, the company paragraph is the only part you must personalise for every firm, and the closing states availability and requests an interview. Keep that skeleton and your letter will always be structurally correct.

Nursing Ausbildung Motivation Letter Example

For nursing (Pflegefachmann / Pflegefachfrau) you need a warm, trustworthy tone and strong German, usually B2. Emphasise empathy, practical experience with people, and your long-term commitment to working in care.

Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,

mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Anzeige für den Ausbildungsplatz als Pflegefachfrau ab [Monat Jahr] gelesen. Der Beruf der Pflegefachkraft verbindet medizinisches Wissen mit menschlicher Nähe, genau das möchte ich in meiner beruflichen Zukunft tun.

In meinem Heimatland habe ich die [Schulname] mit dem Schwerpunkt [Fach] abgeschlossen und anschließend ein Praktikum in einer Senioreneinrichtung absolviert. Dort habe ich gelernt, geduldig zuzuhören, auf Menschen einzugehen und auch in stressigen Situationen ruhig zu bleiben. Ich spreche Deutsch auf dem Niveau B2 und verbessere mich weiter.

Ihre Einrichtung überzeugt mich, weil [konkreter Grund, z. B. die enge Zusammenarbeit mit Auszubildenden / das familiäre Team / die moderne Ausstattung]. Ich möchte die dreijährige Ausbildung erfolgreich abschließen und dauerhaft in Deutschland im Pflegeberuf arbeiten.

Über ein Vorstellungsgespräch freue ich mich sehr.

Mit freundlichen Grüßen

[Vorname Nachname]

What makes this example strong: it mentions a concrete B2 language level (the employer's biggest worry), shows hands-on experience with people, and explicitly promises to stay after training. If you have a Goethe-Zertifikat or telc certificate, name it and attach it.

IT Ausbildung Motivation Letter Example

For IT roles such as Fachinformatiker für Anwendungsentwicklung, highlight logic, projects you have built, and self-study. German employers love proof, so mention a small project, a language you code in, or a certificate.

Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,

Ihre Suche nach einem Auszubildenden zum Fachinformatiker für Anwendungsentwicklung ab [Monat Jahr] hat mein großes Interesse geweckt. Programmieren ist seit Jahren meine Leidenschaft, und ich möchte diese Begeisterung in eine professionelle Ausbildung verwandeln.

Ich habe die mittlere Reife mit guten Noten in Mathematik und Informatik abgeschlossen und mir Kenntnisse in [z. B. HTML, CSS, Python] im Selbststudium beigebracht. In meiner Freizeit habe ich ein kleines Projekt entwickelt: [kurze Beschreibung des Projekts]. Dabei habe ich gelernt, strukturiert zu arbeiten und Probleme systematisch zu lösen. Mein Deutsch liegt auf dem Niveau B1/B2.

Besonders an Ihrem Unternehmen gefällt mir, dass [konkreter Grund, z. B. neue Softwarelösungen für Kunden / ein strukturiertes Ausbildungsprogramm]. Ich möchte meine Kenntnisse bei Ihnen vertiefen und mich langfristig in Ihrem Team weiterentwickeln.

Über ein Gespräch freue ich mich sehr und bin ab [Datum] verfügbar.

Mit freundlichen Grüßen

[Vorname Nachname]

Tip: attach a short link to your project or GitHub profile if you have one. It makes the "I have taught myself to code" claim verifiable, which is exactly what a technical recruiter wants to see.

German Language Tips

Your German does not need to be perfect, but it must be correct and formal. Here are the phrases that carry the most weight in an Ausbildung letter:

  • Opening: "mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Anzeige für den Ausbildungsplatz als [Beruf] gelesen."
  • Background: "In den Fächern [Fach] und [Fach] erziele ich gute Leistungen." / "Ein Praktikum bei [Firma] hat mir gezeigt, dass..."
  • Strengths: "Ich arbeite zuverlässig, lerne schnell und bin es gewohnt, im Team zu arbeiten."
  • Company fit: "Ihr Unternehmen überzeugt mich durch [Grund]."
  • Closing: "Über eine Einladung zu einem persönlichen Gespräch freue ich mich sehr." / "Mit freundlichen Grüßen"

Useful vocabulary: der Ausbildungsplatz (training place), die Bewerbung (application), das Vorstellungsgespräch (interview), die Berufsschule (vocational school), der Betrieb (company), die Ausbildungsvergütung (training salary), der Abschluss (qualification).

If your German is below B1, invest time in learning first. The Goethe-Zertifikat and telc examinations are the two most recognised German certificates for employers, and both are offered worldwide.

Recruiters would rather read honest, correct B1 German than perfect English that signals you are not ready for a German workplace. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I write a German motivation letter for Ausbildung?

Write in the formal German style, open by stating the exact apprenticeship you are applying for, then explain your school background, practical experience, why this occupation, why this company, and your future plans. Keep it to one page (roughly 300-500 words), print it, sign it, and send it with your CV and school certificates.

How long should an Ausbildung motivation letter be?

One page, usually 300-500 words. German employers expect a concise letter, not an essay. A single DIN A4 page with 3-5 short paragraphs is the accepted standard.

Can I write my motivation letter in English?

Technically yes, some companies accept English, but your chances are much higher if you write in German. Even basic German (B1) shows motivation and reduces the employer's risk. If your German is still weak, use simple, correct German rather than a polished letter with errors.

Is a motivation letter required for Ausbildung?

Most German companies require a full application that includes a motivation letter (Anschreiben or Motivationsschreiben), a CV (Lebenslauf), and copies of school certificates. A few small firms only ask for a CV, but you should always include a letter because it is the standard for vocational training applications.

What should be included in an Ausbildung motivation letter?

Include your contact details, the position and start date, why you chose this occupation, relevant school subjects and grades, internships or practical experience, why you chose this specific company, your personal strengths, and your availability. Always close with a polite request for an interview.

What is the German word for motivation letter?

The most common terms are "Motivationsschreiben" and "Anschreiben". In practice, German employers use them almost interchangeably for vocational training applications, though "Anschreiben" is the standard name used on the application (Bewerbung).

What German level do I need for Ausbildung?

Most employers expect at least B1 German for office, trade, and technical roles, and B2 for healthcare and nursing professions. The company decides the exact requirement, and the Federal Employment Agency lists language requirements for each advertised training place.

How should I address an Ausbildung motivation letter?

Use "Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren" if you do not know the contact person, or "Sehr geehrte Frau [Name]" / "Sehr geehrter Herr [Name]" when a name is given in the job advertisement. Never use "Hallo" or first names in a formal application letter.

What should I write in the opening paragraph?

State the exact apprenticeship and start date, and show you read the advertisement: "mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Anzeige für den Ausbildungsplatz als Fachinformatiker ab August 2027 gelesen." One short sentence is enough; do not copy a template phrase without personalising it.

How long is an Ausbildung in Germany?

Most dual vocational training programmes last 2 to 3.5 years. Two-year programmes include some auxiliary professions, while three to three-and-a-half-year programmes cover most recognised occupations such as electrician, mechanic, IT specialist, and nurse.

Do international trainees get paid during Ausbildung?

Yes. Trainees receive a monthly training allowance (Ausbildungsvergütung). For 2026 the minimum apprentice wage is around 682 EUR in the first year, rising in later years; many companies pay more, and collective agreements set higher rates for many occupations.

Is Ausbildung free for international applicants?

Yes. The company pays you, and the public vocational school (Berufsschule) is free. Unlike university studies, there is no tuition fee for Ausbildung, though you must cover living costs and prove you can finance your stay for the training visa.

How far in advance should I apply for Ausbildung?

Apply 9 to 12 months before the start date. Many large companies open applications for the following autumn in August to October, and popular employers close early. Applying early gives you time to improve your German and prepare for interviews.

What documents go with the Ausbildung motivation letter?

Your CV (Lebenslauf) in reverse-chronological format, your most recent school certificates and grade reports, German language certificates (for example Goethe-Zertifikat or telc), and any internship or work references. Some companies ask for a passport photo on the CV, as is German convention.

Can I do Ausbildung without speaking German?

In practice, no. The Berufsschule and the workplace are in German, so you need at least B1, and B2 is strongly preferred for nursing and healthcare. You can learn German first and then apply; some companies also offer preparatory language courses.

What is the difference between an Ausbildung letter and a university motivation letter?

A university motivation letter focuses on academic interests and career ambitions. An Ausbildung letter is more practical: it must prove reliability, hands-on skills, an understanding of the trade, and a genuine intention to complete the whole apprenticeship in Germany.

What do German employers look for in a motivation letter?

They look for reliability, honesty, practical experience, a realistic understanding of the profession, and a long-term commitment to staying in Germany. Unrealistic claims, copied template phrases, spelling errors, and missing documents are the most common reasons applications are rejected.

Should I use formal or informal German in my motivation letter?

Always formal German. Use "Sie" forms, "Sehr geehrte" as the salutation, and "Mit freundlichen Grüßen" as the closing. Informal language is a quick way to be rejected.

Can I apply for Ausbildung with a school certificate from my home country?

Yes, your foreign school leaving certificate can be accepted, but you may need a translation and, for some professions, an official recognition statement (Zeugnisbewertung). The recognition portal anerkennung-in-deutschland.de explains the process for your country.

What happens after Ausbildung — can I stay in Germany?

Yes. After completing Ausbildung you can stay in Germany to work in your trained profession. Trainees who finish a recognised apprenticeship are among the profiles the German government actively wants to keep, and you can apply for a residence permit for skilled workers.

How do I send my Ausbildung application?

Follow the instructions in the advertisement. Many companies now accept a single PDF with your letter, CV, and certificates. If you send by email, use a clear subject such as "Bewerbung Ausbildung als [Beruf] - [Vorname Nachname]". If posting, print everything and sign the letter by hand.

Should I mention my age or visa status in the letter?

You do not need to state your age in the letter, but for international applicants it is smart to briefly mention your language level and your permission or plan to obtain a training visa. This answers the employer's main concern before they even ask.

Can I use the same letter for every company?

No. A generic letter signals laziness. German recruiters quickly spot mass-produced applications. Personalise at least the company-specific paragraph for every application, and keep a small database of the reasons you give for each firm.

What salary can I expect during Ausbildung?

The legal minimum apprentice wage for 2026 is roughly 682 EUR per month in the first year, with automatic increases in the second and third years. Many collective agreements pay between 800 and 1,200 EUR, and some large companies pay more.

Do I need a visa for an Ausbildung in Germany?

Yes, non-EU citizens need a vocational training visa. You must have a training contract with the employer, prove German language skills, and show you can cover your living costs. Official guidance is on the Make it in Germany website and the German missions in your country.

  • Have I explained why I chose this profession and this specific company?
  • Have I stated my German level and my visa plan (if international)?
  • Is the tone modest, factual, and written in correct German?
  • Have I avoided all spelling and grammar errors (especially umlauts)?
  • Is the closing polite and does it request an interview?
  • Is my CV and school certificates attached as one clean PDF?
