Study in Japan 2026: Complete Guide for International Students

Japan combines some of Asia's best universities with generous government funding and a booming demand for international talent. With the fully funded MEXT scholarship, national university tuition from about JPY 535,800 per year, and hundreds of English-taught programmes, Japan is one of the most attractive study destinations in the region. This guide covers universities, costs, funding, visas, and the application timeline so you can plan every step.

Why study in Japan?

Japan is a global leader in technology, engineering, and scientific research, with universities that consistently rank among the best in Asia and the world. For international students, it offers a rare combination of high academic quality and accessible funding.

  • Fully funded MEXT Japanese Government Scholarships for all degree levels
  • Affordable national university tuition from about JPY 535,800 per year
  • Hundreds of English-taught degree programmes, especially at the graduate level
  • World-leading research in robotics, materials, AI, biotechnology, and more
  • Safe cities, efficient public transport, and a rich cultural environment
  • Strong post-study job market and support for international graduates

Key numbers

  • National university tuition: about JPY 535,800 per year
  • Private university tuition: JPY 800,000 to JPY 2,000,000 per year
  • Living costs: JPY 100,000 to JPY 180,000 per month
  • Part-time work: up to 28 hours per week during term

Tuition fees and living costs

Studying in Japan is more affordable than in most Western destinations, especially at national universities, which charge a standardized annual tuition.

Tuition fees

  • National universities: about JPY 535,800 per year (standard national rate)
  • Public universities: similar to national universities, typically JPY 500,000 to JPY 600,000
  • Private universities: JPY 800,000 to JPY 2,000,000 per year depending on the faculty

Living costs

Living costs depend heavily on location. Tokyo is the most expensive city; regional cities such as Sendai, Sapporo, and Fukuoka are more affordable.

  • Accommodation: JPY 40,000 to JPY 100,000 per month
  • Food and daily expenses: JPY 40,000 to JPY 70,000 per month
  • Total budget: roughly JPY 100,000 to JPY 180,000 per month

Scholarships and funding for international students

Japan is one of the most scholarship-friendly study destinations. The main funding routes are:

  • MEXT (Japanese Government) Scholarship: fully funded, covering tuition, a monthly stipend, and travel, for undergraduate, graduate, and research students.
  • Embassy recommendation: apply through the Japanese embassy in your home country, with country-specific deadlines and screening.
  • University recommendation: apply directly through a partner Japanese university, often with higher chances at specific institutions.
  • JASSO scholarships: Japan Student Services Organization awards for international students, including the Monbukagakusho Honors Scholarship.
  • University and private scholarships: many universities and foundations offer merit or need-based awards to international students.

English-taught programmes in Japan

Japan's Top Global University (SGU) initiative supports dozens of universities in offering English-taught degree programmes, with the widest selection at the master's and PhD level. Popular fields include engineering, business, computer science, data science, international relations, and sustainability.

English-taught programmes usually require an English proficiency certificate such as IELTS or TOEFL. Some universities also accept the Duolingo English Test. For Japanese-taught programmes, you typically need a JLPT certificate at N2 level or higher. Review the Duolingo English Test guide and the IELTS guide if you need help preparing.

Student visa for Japan

International students studying in Japan for more than 90 days need a student visa based on a Certificate of Eligibility. The process differs from most other countries because your university handles the first step.

Steps to get a student visa

  1. Receive an official acceptance (offer of admission) from a Japanese university.
  2. The university applies for a Certificate of Eligibility at the regional immigration office on your behalf.
  3. You receive the Certificate of Eligibility and submit it with your passport to the Japanese embassy or consulate in your home country.
  4. The embassy issues a student visa, usually within a few weeks.
  5. Arrive in Japan and register your residence at your local city office.

Processing times vary, so start the process two to three months before your programme begins. For a general walkthrough, see the student visa guide.

Working while studying in Japan

International students can work part time in Japan after obtaining a work permit (shikakugai katsudo kyoka) from the immigration office.

  • During term: up to 28 hours per week
  • During long holidays: up to 40 hours per week
  • Typical roles: retail, cafés, language tutoring, and university research assistance

Many universities also offer on-campus jobs and part-time research assistant positions. Balance work with your studies, since the primary purpose of your visa is study.

Admission requirements and timeline

Most Japanese universities run two main intakes: April and October. Graduate schools often have additional rolling rounds.

Study in Japan frequently asked questions

Can I study in Japan without knowing Japanese?

Yes. Many Japanese universities offer English-taught degree programmes, especially at the graduate level and through the Top Global University (SGU) initiative. Learning basic Japanese is still recommended for daily life.

How much does it cost to study in Japan?

National universities charge about JPY 535,800 per year, while private universities charge JPY 800,000 to JPY 2,000,000. Living costs range from JPY 100,000 to JPY 180,000 per month. MEXT and other scholarships can cover these costs.

What is the MEXT scholarship and how do I apply?

The MEXT (Japanese Government) Scholarship is fully funded, covering tuition, a monthly stipend, and travel. Apply through embassy recommendation via the Japanese embassy in your home country, or university recommendation directly through a Japanese university.

What are the top universities in Japan?

The University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, Osaka University, Institute of Science Tokyo, Tohoku University, Nagoya University, Hokkaido University, and Kyushu University are the leading national universities, alongside private institutions Waseda and Keio.

Can I work while studying in Japan?

Yes. International students on a student visa can work up to 28 hours per week during term and up to 40 hours per week during long holidays, after obtaining a work permit from immigration.

Do I need the JLPT to study in Japan?

The JLPT is required for Japanese-taught programmes, typically at N2 or higher. English-taught programmes usually require IELTS or TOEFL instead.

How do I get a student visa for Japan?

Your university applies for a Certificate of Eligibility at immigration after you are accepted. You use it to apply for a student visa at the Japanese embassy or consulate in your home country.

Are there English-taught programmes in Japan?

Yes. The Top Global University (SGU) initiative supports English-taught degrees at dozens of universities, with the widest selection at the graduate level in engineering, business, computer science, and international studies.

When should I apply to study in Japan?

Most universities admit students in April and October. April intake applications usually close between October and December; October intake between April and June. MEXT embassy-track deadlines vary by country.

Is Japan a good destination for international students?

Yes. Japan offers top universities, generous funding like MEXT, safe cities, a strong technology sector, and growing post-study career opportunities for international graduates.
