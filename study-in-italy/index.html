Study in Italy 2026: Complete Guide for International Students

Italy combines one of the world's oldest university traditions with affordable, high-quality education. With public tuition starting around EUR 900 per year, more than 500 English-taught programmes, and generous Italian government scholarships, Italy remains one of Europe's most popular study destinations for international students. This guide covers universities, costs, funding, and the visa process so you can plan every step.

Why study in Italy?

Italy is home to the University of Bologna, founded in 1088 and widely considered the oldest university in the Western world. Today the country offers a distinctive combination of academic heritage, affordable education, and strong international rankings.

  • Affordable public tuition from about EUR 900 per year
  • Over 500 English-taught degree programmes
  • World-renowned universities in design, architecture, engineering, and the arts
  • Italian government and regional scholarships for international students
  • Rich cultural heritage and strategic gateway to Europe
  • Post-study stay options for job seeking and work

Key numbers

  • Public tuition: EUR 900 to EUR 4,000 per year
  • Private tuition: EUR 6,000 to EUR 20,000 per year
  • Living costs: EUR 700 to EUR 1,200 per month
  • English-taught programmes: 500+

Tuition fees and living costs

Italy is one of the most affordable study destinations in Western Europe. Public universities set fees progressively based on family income, so students with lower household income often pay significantly less than the maximum.

Tuition fees

  • Public universities: EUR 900 to EUR 4,000 per year, scaled to family income
  • Private universities: EUR 6,000 to EUR 20,000 per year
  • Some regions grant full fee waivers to international students on scholarships

Living costs

Living costs vary by city. Milan and Rome are the most expensive; smaller cities like Padua, Pavia, and Bologna are more affordable.

  • Accommodation: EUR 300 to EUR 700 per month
  • Food and transport: EUR 250 to EUR 400 per month
  • Total budget: roughly EUR 700 to EUR 1,200 per month

Scholarships and funding for international students

Several funding routes can reduce or eliminate the cost of studying in Italy. The most important are:

  • Italian government scholarships (MAECI): funding for international students covering tuition, a monthly stipend, and health insurance for bachelor's, master's, and PhD studies.
  • Regional DSU scholarships: need-based funding managed by regional bodies such as Lazio Disco, Ergo, and Edisu, covering tuition, meals, and housing.
  • University merit scholarships: many Italian universities award fee waivers or stipends to high-achieving international applicants.
  • Invest Your Talent in Italy: a fully funded programme for English-taught master's degrees.
  • Part-time work: students may work up to 20 hours per week during term time.

English-taught programmes in Italy

Italy offers more than 500 degree programmes taught entirely in English, with the strongest selection at the master's level. Popular fields include engineering, business and economics, computer science, design, international relations, medicine, and data science.

Admission to English-taught programmes usually requires an English proficiency certificate such as IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo English Test. Some universities accept applicants without a formal test if the previous degree was taught in English. If English tests are a barrier, review the scholarships without IELTS guide and the Duolingo English Test guide.

Student visa for Italy

Non-EU students studying in Italy for more than 90 days need a national D-type study visa, issued by the Italian embassy or consulate in their home country. Plan for visa processing times of several weeks, and apply only after receiving an official university acceptance.

Documents typically required

  1. Valid passport with at least two blank pages
  2. Official university acceptance or pre-enrollment letter
  3. Proof of financial means for at least EUR 460 per month
  4. Proof of accommodation in Italy
  5. Health insurance covering the Schengen area
  6. Completed visa application form and photos

After arrival, apply for a residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) within eight days at the local police station. For a complete walkthrough of the process, see the student visa guide.

Study in Italy frequently asked questions

Can I study in Italy in English?

Yes. Italy offers more than 500 English-taught degree programmes, particularly at the graduate level. Universities like Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, and the University of Bologna have extensive English-taught offerings.

How much does it cost to study in Italy?

Tuition at public universities ranges from about EUR 900 to EUR 4,000 per year depending on family income. Private universities charge EUR 6,000 to EUR 20,000 per year. Living costs range from EUR 700 to EUR 1,200 per month.

What scholarships are available for studying in Italy?

Italian government scholarships (MAECI), regional DSU scholarships, university merit scholarships, and the Invest Your Talent in Italy programme are the main options. See the scholarships in Italy page for verified open programs.

Do I need to speak Italian to study in Italy?

For English-taught programmes, no Italian is required for admission, though basic Italian is strongly recommended for daily life. For Italian-taught programmes, you typically need B2 level Italian or higher.

How do I get a student visa for Italy?

Apply for a national D-type study visa at the Italian embassy or consulate after receiving your university acceptance. You need proof of enrollment, financial means, accommodation, and health insurance.

Is it expensive to study in Italy for international students?

Italy is one of the most affordable study destinations in Europe. Public universities charge as little as EUR 900 per year, and scholarships and part-time work can offset living costs.

What are the top universities in Italy?

The University of Bologna, Sapienza University of Rome, Politecnico di Milano, University of Padua, University of Milan, and Bocconi University are among the most highly ranked and internationally recognized Italian universities.

Can I work while studying in Italy?

Yes. Non-EU students on a study visa may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full time during holidays. A residence permit allowing work is required.
